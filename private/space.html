04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Stoke Space was still working to close its ~$1B raise as of late August on top of $1.34B already spent pre-launch; dilution and debt are chronic among small caps (RKLB's $3.6B Iridium bridge facility, ASTS's $1B convertible notes) — and ASTS's 15.5% single-day drop on Fed rate-hike chatter (Aug 28) shows how exposed debt-funded builders are to the rate cycle.
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue is NASA/DoD — the SDA's Golden Dome build-out (L3Harris/Sierra Space's $1.75B Tranche 3 award, RKLB's $397M Flatellite and $981M NITE-STAR ceiling) keeps flowing, but the Aug 17 CEO ouster at prime contractor L3Harris — followed by a Deutsche Bank price-target cut — is a reminder that governance and sentiment risk sit alongside budget risk in this concentrated customer base.
SpaceX absorbs the value / sector repricing
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX has recovered above its $135 IPO price but remains ~37% below its post-IPO high, and its next real catalyst (Starship Flight 14) just slipped from Aug 28 to a mid/late-September window. Meanwhile record fundamentals across RKLB, RDW, LUNR and Firefly this earnings season haven't stopped RKLB from closing nearly 57% off its 52-week high — a widening gap between operating results and price that cuts both ways.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or delayed launch can halve a small-cap, or push a manifest by months: Starship Flight 14 has already slipped from Aug 28 to a Sept 15-30 window as Ship 41 and Booster 21 complete static-fire testing, Rocket Lab's Neutron still hasn't flown, and Blue Origin's BE-4 root-cause finding shows how fast hardware failure can stall a program ahead of its promised year-end return to flight. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
