04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Stoke Space is targeting a close this month on its $1B raise on top of $1.34B already spent pre-launch; dilution is chronic among small caps (RKLB's $3.6B Iridium bridge facility, ASTS's $1B convertible notes).
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue is NASA/DoD — the SDA's Golden Dome build-out (L3Harris/Sierra Space's $1.75B Tranche 3 award, RKLB's $981M NITE-STAR ceiling) keeps flowing, but a CEO ouster at prime contractor L3Harris is a reminder that governance risk sits alongside budget risk in this concentrated customer base.
SpaceX absorbs the value / sector repricing
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX dipped back below its $135 IPO price around the Aug 20 share unlock and remains ~39% below its post-IPO high. More broadly, record fundamentals across RKLB, RDW, LUNR and FLY this earnings season haven't stopped a growth-stock/space-stock selloff that's pulled RKLB over 50% off its 52-week high — a widening gap between operating results and price that cuts both ways.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or launch can halve a small-cap, or push a manifest a year: Rocket Lab's Neutron — already delayed once by a tank failure, then flagged again by Bloomberg on Aug 10 — still hasn't flown, while Blue Origin's BE-4 root-cause finding shows how fast hardware failure can stall a program ahead of its promised year-end return to flight. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
