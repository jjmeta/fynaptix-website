04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Stoke Space is now raising a further $1B on top of $1.34B already spent pre-launch; dilution is chronic among small caps (RKLB's $3.6B Iridium bridge facility, ASTS's $1B convertible notes).
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue is NASA/DoD — SDA's Golden Dome build-out is driving billions in LHX/Sierra Space awards and a fresh $397M RKLB Flatellites contract; budget shifts or program cancellations reprice backlogs fast.
SpaceX absorbs the value
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX shares needed a rebound above the $135 IPO price just to stop the bleeding and remain ~38% below their post-IPO high; xAI's AI cash burn continues to weigh on consolidated results, and Musk is now promising Starship Flight 14 will both catch the ship and deliver Starlink V3 on the same flight — a high bar.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or launch can halve a small-cap, or push a manifest a year: Bloomberg reported Aug 10 that Rocket Lab's Neutron — already delayed once by a tank failure — now faces fresh risk of slipping from Q4 2026 into 2027, while Blue Origin's own root-cause finding (a single BE-4 oxygen valve) shows how fast hardware failure can stall a program. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
