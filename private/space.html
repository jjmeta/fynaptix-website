04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Stoke Space's Series E closed at ~$1B on Aug 21 on top of $1.34B already spent pre-launch; dilution and debt are chronic among small caps (RKLB's $3.6B Iridium bridge facility, ASTS's $1B convertible notes), and ASTS is only now recovering from a 15.5% single-day drop on Fed rate-hike chatter — a reminder of how exposed debt-funded builders are to the rate cycle even as this week's rally shows sentiment can turn just as fast.
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue is NASA/DoD — the SDA's Golden Dome build-out (L3Harris/Sierra Space's $1.75B Tranche 3 award, RKLB's $397M Flatellite and $981M NITE-STAR ceiling) keeps flowing, but a securities-claims investigation now sits alongside the Aug 17 CEO ouster at prime contractor L3Harris, a reminder that governance and sentiment risk sit alongside budget risk in this concentrated customer base.
SpaceX absorbs the value / sector repricing
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX has climbed to ~$149.74 but remains ~34% below its post-IPO high, and its next real catalyst (Starship Flight 14) is still NET Sept 15-30. Meanwhile record fundamentals across RKLB, RDW, LUNR and Firefly this earnings season haven't stopped RKLB shares from trading below the $67.50 floor of its own Iridium collar — a widening gap between operating results and price that cuts both ways.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or delayed launch can halve a small-cap, or push a manifest by months: Starship Flight 14 remains NET Sept 15-30 even after Ship 41's full-duration six-engine static fire, Rocket Lab's Neutron still hasn't flown despite 400+ hot fires, and Blue Origin's BE-4 root-cause finding shows how fast hardware failure can stall a program ahead of its promised year-end return to flight. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
