04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Rocket Lab's Iridium financing alone required a $1.94B dilutive ATM offering on top of the earlier bridge facility, and Stoke Space's $1B Series E (total raised $2.3B) is funding a Nova Block 2 that isn't now targeted until 2029; ASTS's $1B convertible notes remain a separate overhang.
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue still runs through NASA/DoD — L3Harris just landed a new $4.7B Lockheed PAC-3 MSE contract and Guggenheim initiated it at Buy, yet shares remain stuck near 52-week lows with a securities-claims investigation unresolved; the Pentagon's SHIELD IDIQ vehicle has ballooned to 2,400+ qualified vendors, diluting any one company's edge in winning Golden Dome work.
SpaceX absorbs the value / sector repricing
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX sits ~32% below its post-IPO high and even its own marquee catalyst keeps slipping: Starship Flight 14 has now moved from Sept 15 to Sept 18/19 to NET Sept 28. Meanwhile RDW's record Q2 didn't stop a fresh $1.15B insider-selling disclosure and a 7% one-day drop, another sign of the gap between operating results and price across the sector.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or slipped date can move a small-cap sharply: Starship Flight 14 has slipped three times in three weeks, Stoke's Nova Pathfinder is already pushed to early 2027, Neutron still looks pointed at 2027 after January's tank-pressure-test rupture, and Blue Origin's New Glenn return-to-flight target faces open skepticism from SpaceX veterans. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
