04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Capital intensity
Rockets and constellations burn billions before returns — Stoke Space's Series E closed at $1B on Sept 8 (total raised now $2.3B) to fund Nova Pathfinder, whose first flight has already slipped from 'end of 2026' to 'early 2027'; dilution and debt stay chronic among small caps (RKLB's Iridium bridge facility, ASTS's $1B convertible notes).
Government concentration
Much of the sector's revenue is NASA/DoD — L3Harris just landed a new $4.7B Lockheed contract for PAC-3 MSE interceptor propulsion on top of its $1.75B AMDT3 Golden Dome award, yet LHX shares are testing 52-week lows anyway, a reminder that a growing government backlog doesn't guarantee a re-rating while a securities-claims investigation sits unresolved.
SpaceX absorbs the value / sector repricing
A dominant incumbent with launch-cost leverage can compress everyone else's margins — SPCX has climbed back to $151.21 but remains ~33% below its post-IPO high, and even its own near-term catalyst has slipped: Starship Flight 14 moved from a Sept 15 target to Sept 18/19. Meanwhile RKLB's record Q2 didn't stop shares from sitting 58%+ off their highs, a widening gap between operating results and price across the sector.
Binary mission risk
One failed test or slipped date can move a small-cap sharply: Stoke's Nova Pathfinder just slipped from year-end 2026 to early 2027, Neutron still looks pointed at 2027 after January's tank-pressure-test rupture, and Blue Origin's New Glenn return-to-flight target faces open skepticism from SpaceX veterans over the pad-rebuild timeline. SpaceX's own prospectus flags "key man" dependence on Elon Musk.
