03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic — and this week's best news is still early
Zero AI-discovered drugs have reached full FDA approval. Recursion's TUPELO data, Generate's Phase 3 entry and Lilly's VERVE-102 durability are all genuine progress — but they're Phase 1-3 readouts, not approvals, and the field's clearest recent clinical win (Intellia's HAELO, now a rolling BLA) still isn't an AI-discovery story.
IP litigation is now a multi-year balance-sheet risk, not a footnote
Guardant Health has formally appealed the $245.2M TwinStrand judgment to the Federal Circuit while separately pursuing PTAB challenges to the underlying patents — two parallel tracks that could run for years, even with execution stayed in the meantime. Its own offensive suit against Tempus still has a Markman hearing pending Oct 20. Patent risk cuts in both directions in the diagnostics layer, and it moves real money either way.
Catalyst-driven volatility, in both directions
Generate Biomedicines rallied to an Aug 25 all-time high of $20.38, then gave back most of that move to sit at $16.39 barely a week and a half later — with no change to the underlying GB-0895 Phase 3 program either way. Relay has cooled off its own highs too. Proof these stocks can re-rate violently on sentiment and single data points alone, detached from the clinical facts — in a portfolio, that volatility cuts both ways.
Hardware and instrument risk isn't just a biology problem
Quantum-Si confirmed the Proteus launch is now a full year late (pushed to Q2 2027) and is cutting 20% of its workforce to fund the delay, extending cash runway to Q4 2028 — buying time, but not shipping the product. Compare that to Abbott's ~$21B all-cash purchase of Exact Sciences: real test volumes and reimbursed CPT codes command real M&A premiums, while unproven instrument platforms carry genuine execution risk regardless of the underlying science.
Funding is recovering, but selectively
H1 2026 venture funding reached $16.3B across 235 rounds and M&A already topped all of 2025 — but roughly two-thirds of those venture rounds still went to biotechs that already had a drug in human testing. Early-formation platforms remain comparatively starved of capital even as headline totals hit multi-year highs, and long-duration rate sensitivity can close the funding window unevenly.
