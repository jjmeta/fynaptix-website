03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic — and the AI-drug approval math just got longer
Zero AI-discovered drugs have reached full FDA approval, out of 200+ now in clinical development. Insilico's own lead investigator now puts rentosertib's realistic timeline at 2029-2030 (three to four years from Phase III start), not the 2027-28 window previously discussed — a reminder that even the field's clearest proof point is still years, not quarters, away.
Diagnostics litigation remains a three-way mess with no resolution in sight
No new movement this week on any of the three active fronts: Guardant's suit against Tempus (surviving dismissal since Aug 27), Tempus's dismissed-with-prejudice suit against Guardant (under appeal since Feb), or the $245.2M TwinStrand judgment against Guardant (also under appeal to the Federal Circuit). Three lawsuits, two companies, years of legal spend ahead regardless of outcome.
Sentiment swings faster than the biology, in both directions
Tempus surged roughly 40% in a week on a data-platform announcement with no new clinical or regulatory event; Recursion rebounded 8-9% across two separate days on early, uncontrolled trial interest rather than results. Real moves, thin catalysts — proof this tier can re-rate sharply on narrative alone, which cuts both ways in a portfolio.
A fund's own AUM can shrink even while its price rallies
ARKG closed at a fresh 52-week high this week, yet its AUM fell from $2.00B to $1.93B over the same two weeks — consistent with investors redeeming into strength rather than new money arriving. A concentrated, actively managed fund can lose assets during its best stretch, not just its worst.
Funding is recovering but selectively, and 2026's M&A pace is itself a valuation risk
Average biotech deal size is up 44% y/y to $527M as pharma races to refill a patent-cliff hole worth over $300B in branded revenue this decade (only 4% of global drug sales will remain under patent by 2030, down from 12% in 2022) — but Q1 biopharma VC funding of $5.2B tilted heavily toward later, de-risked rounds with earn-the-back-end milestone structures, not early platform bets. A hot-M&A, friendlier-FDA rally can compress forward returns for anyone buying the rally rather than the underlying science.
