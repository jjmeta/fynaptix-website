03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic
Zero AI-discovered drugs have reached full FDA approval — rentosertib's Phase III initiation (Jul 7) is the field's furthest milestone, not a finish line. Recursion's Q2 revenue fell 60% YoY to $7.67M, missing estimates; management responded by cutting 2026 cash opex guidance to under $375M rather than promising a near-term revenue fix.
Catalyst-driven volatility
These stocks trade on announcements; between catalysts they drift and dilute. Generate Biomedicines missed Q2 estimates and widened its loss, yet H.C. Wainwright raised its price target to $25 from $16 the same day. Eikon added a credible Merck scientific advisor on Aug 10, but that followed two board resignations and an Aug 4 lock-up expiry — shares sit ~48% below IPO with no clinical catalyst yet to offset the overhang.
Hardware and instrument risk isn't just a biology problem
Quantum-Si's Aug 13 disclosure that Proteus won't launch until Q2 2027 — a full year late — alongside a 20% workforce cut, is a reminder that the proteomics layer's timelines slip just like clinical ones. Compare that to Abbott's ~$21B all-cash purchase of Exact Sciences: real test volumes and reimbursed CPT codes command real M&A premiums, while unproven instrument platforms carry genuine execution risk regardless of the underlying science.
Rate sensitivity
Long-duration biotech cash flows are highly sensitive to rates; macro can swamp fundamentals regardless of platform progress. Relay Therapeutics' Aug 6 expansion of its at-the-market equity facility to ~$463M is a reminder that unprofitable platform names fund themselves by diluting shareholders when capital is available, not necessarily when it's cheap.

ARKG's poor returns don't disprove multiomics — the platform costs really did collapse. But they do demonstrate how long "right eventually" can take, and what holding through it costs.
