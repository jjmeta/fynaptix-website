03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic
Zero AI-discovered drugs have reached full FDA approval. Recursion's Q2 loss beat estimates but revenue is still shrinking; even CRISPR Therapeutics' genuinely durable CTX310 data (79% mean ANGPTL3 reduction at 1 year, published in NEJM) is gene editing, not an AI-discovery platform — the field's clearest recent clinical wins keep coming from outside the AI-native names on this page.
IP litigation is now a balance-sheet risk, not a footnote
Guardant Health was hit with a $245.2M final judgment in the TwinStrand/University of Washington patent case (Aug 21, under appeal), the same week its own offensive suit against Tempus survived a dismissal motion but had its Markman hearing pushed from Aug 27 to Oct 20. Patent risk cuts in both directions in the diagnostics layer, and it moves real money either way.
Catalyst-driven volatility, in both directions
Generate Biomedicines swung from trading below its $16 offer to a $19.81 52-week high in the same month, while Relay is +134.8% YTD despite a swollen dilution facility — proof these stocks can re-rate violently on sentiment alone. The Aug 28 session showed the same thing sector-wide: ARKG, XBI and IBB all dropped 2.7-4.5% in a single day even as trailing 1-year returns remain strongly positive.
Hardware and instrument risk isn't just a biology problem
Quantum-Si's Aug 13 disclosure that Proteus won't launch until Q2 2027 — a full year late — alongside a 20% workforce cut, is a reminder that the proteomics layer's timelines slip just like clinical ones. Compare that to Abbott's ~$21B all-cash purchase of Exact Sciences: real test volumes and reimbursed CPT codes command real M&A premiums, while unproven instrument platforms carry genuine execution risk regardless of the underlying science.
Funding is recovering, but selectively
20 biotechs have IPO'd in 2026 (5 in August alone) versus 11 for all of 2025, with 14 raising over $250M — the strongest IPO cohort since 2021 — but about two-thirds of H1 2026 venture rounds still went to biotechs that already had a drug in human testing. Early-formation platforms remain comparatively starved of capital even as headline totals hit multi-year highs, and long-duration rate sensitivity can close the funding window unevenly.

The shrinking-fund problem
As AUM falls, redemptions can force selling of small, illiquid holdings — potentially moving prices against the fund itself. This week's -4.5% single-day move is itself a live example of how fast a concentrated, thinly-traded genomics book can round-trip.
Read it as a signal, not a shopping list
ARKG's historically poor returns don't disprove multiomics — the platform costs really did collapse. But they do demonstrate how long "right eventually" can take, and what holding through it costs.
