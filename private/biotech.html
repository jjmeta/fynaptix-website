03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic
Of 173 AI-discovered programs now in clinical development, zero have reached full FDA approval — rentosertib's advance to Phase III is the field's furthest milestone, not a finish line. Recursion fell ~7.9% on a single unfavorable readout on Jul 16, 2026, a reminder that one good trial does not make field-wide validation.
Catalyst-driven volatility
These stocks trade on announcements; between catalysts they drift and dilute. Relay (RLAY) collected both fresh Buy initiations and a Zacks downgrade to Strong Sell within weeks, then raised $175M in fresh equity. Eikon's (EIKN) IPO lock-up expired Aug 4, 2026, adding a fresh supply overhang right as the stock trades below its Feb 2026 offer price.
Platform-value skepticism
If pharma can license AI tools cheaply (or build in-house), platform companies may capture less value than valuations imply. Anthropic's ~$400M acquihire of 10-person Coefficient Bio (Apr 2026) shows biotech AI capability can sometimes be bought cheaply outside pharma's traditional deal structures — a soft signal against paying platform-scale premiums. The broader AI trade faces its own version of this: hyperscalers' ~$660–690B in planned 2026 infrastructure spend dwarfs the AI software revenue it's meant to support.
Rate sensitivity
Long-duration biotech cash flows are highly sensitive to rates; macro can swamp fundamentals regardless of platform progress.

Where it meets this page

The shrinking-fund problem
As AUM falls, redemptions can force selling of small, illiquid holdings — potentially moving prices against the fund itself. A structural headwind that has nothing to do with whether the science works.

ARKG's poor returns don't disprove multiomics — the platform costs really did collapse. But they do demonstrate how long "right eventually" can take, and what holding through it costs.
