03 // THE MULTIOMICS STACK
Reading Biology at Scale
Multiomics is the data layer underneath everything else on this page: cheap, high-volume molecular measurement is what makes AI-native drug discovery possible at all. Framework and figures drawn partly from ARK's Big Ideas 2026 multiomics chapter.
GENOMICS
The blueprint
DNA sequencing — the base layer. Whole-genome costs have collapsed from thousands to low hundreds of dollars, turning sequencing from a project into a routine input.
EPIGENOMICS
The control layer
Chemical marks that switch genes on and off — the target of reprogramming approaches like NewLimit's. Explains why identical genomes age differently.
TRANSCRIPTOMICS
What the cell is doing now
RNA readouts capture active gene expression rather than static potential — the dynamic signal AI models learn disease states from.
PROTEOMICS
The machinery
Proteins do the actual work of cells and are most drug targets. Next-gen protein sequencing (e.g. Quantum-Si) is the least mature, highest-upside layer of the stack.
METABOLOMICS
The output
Sugars, fats and small molecules reflecting real-time cellular activity — mass-spec tooling (e.g. 908 Devices) is the instrument layer here.
THE FLYWHEEL
Why the layers compound
ARK's core argument: more data → better models → better tools, tests and treatments → more data. Multiomics is where AI's returns to scale show up in biology rather than in text.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Biology still fails in the clinic — even Intellia's milestone is a step, not a finish line
Zero AI-discovered drugs have reached full FDA approval, out of 200+ now in clinical development. Intellia's lonvo-z BLA acceptance with Priority Review (Sept 8) is genuine regulatory progress for in-vivo CRISPR editing — but it's a gene-editing program, not an AI-discovery one, and analysts still peg only ~60% odds that any AI-discovered drug (most likely Insilico's rentosertib) reaches approval before 2027-28.
IP litigation now runs in both directions, and neither side is close to done
A Delaware federal judge refused to dismiss Guardant's infringement suit against Tempus on Aug 27, keeping that fight alive — while Guardant is separately appealing the $245.2M TwinStrand judgment against it to the Federal Circuit, with parallel PTAB challenges still pending. Two companies, two active fronts each, years of legal spend ahead regardless of the underlying diagnostics business.
Catalyst-driven volatility, in both directions
Recursion keeps drifting to fresh lows (now $3.21, near the 52-week low of $2.77) on no new negative data, while Generate's round trip from its Aug 25 high has at least stabilized. Proof these stocks can re-rate on sentiment and single data points alone, detached from the clinical facts — in a portfolio, that volatility cuts both ways.
Hardware and instrument risk isn't just a biology problem
Quantum-Si's Proteus launch is still a full year late (Q2 2027) after the confirmed delay and 20% workforce cut, with no new resolving catalyst this week — a June partnership with Cell Signaling Technology on protein validation is background work, not a shipping product. Compare that to Illumina's return to the S&P 500 on the back of real, beating revenue: instrument platforms carry genuine execution risk that a strong dataset alone doesn't fix.
Funding is recovering, but selectively — and the rally itself is a risk if it's gotten ahead of the data
Industry M&A topped $100B in H1 2026 (best pace since before the pandemic) as pharma races to refill a ~$300B patent cliff by 2028, and XBI is nearing its first all-time high since 2021 — but roughly two-thirds of H1's venture rounds still went to biotechs that already had a drug in human testing. A friendlier-FDA, hot-M&A rally can compress forward returns for anyone buying in now, and long-duration rate sensitivity can close the funding window unevenly.
