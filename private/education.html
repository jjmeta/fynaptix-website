EDUCATION // INVESTING 101

Learn to Invest

From first principles to stocks, ETFs & crypto — a practical, honest guide

Evergreen guide · Updated Jul 2026
Educational content — not financial advice. This guide explains concepts so you can make your own informed decisions. Investing involves risk of loss; nothing here is a recommendation for any specific investment. Rules on brokers, tax, and crypto differ by country — check your local situation and consider a licensed advisor.
01 // FIRST PRINCIPLES
Why Investing Works at All
COMPOUNDING
Time beats timing
£500/month at 8% average returns is ~£75k after 10 years, ~£745k after 30. The last decade contributes more than the first two combined — starting early matters more than starting big.
RISK = RETURN
There's no free lunch
Higher expected return always means higher risk of loss or volatility. Anyone promising high returns with low risk is mistaken or selling something.
DIVERSIFICATION
The only free-ish lunch
Owning many uncorrelated things reduces risk more than it reduces return. It's why index funds beat most stock-pickers over time.
COSTS & TAX
What you keep is what counts
Fees and taxes compound against you exactly like returns compound for you. Low-cost funds and tax-advantaged accounts are the two highest-certainty "returns" available.
BEHAVIOR
You are the biggest risk
The average investor underperforms the funds they own — by buying high (excitement) and selling low (fear). Systems beat willpower: automate contributions, write decisions down (Journal).
ORDER OF OPERATIONS
Before investing anything
1) Emergency fund (3–6 months). 2) Pay off high-interest debt. 3) Fill tax-advantaged accounts. 4) Then invest — see Portfolio Strategy for allocation.
02 // HOW MARKETS WORK
The Mechanics in Plain Language
ConceptWhat it actually means
ShareA tiny ownership slice of a real business — its profits, growth, and risks. Not a lottery ticket with a chart attached.
Price vs valuePrice is what the last buyer paid; value is what the business's future cash flows are worth. Prices swing far more than values do — that gap is both the danger and the opportunity.
Market & limit ordersMarket = buy now at whatever the price is. Limit = buy only at your price or better. For anything thinly traded (small caps, some ETFs), always use limits.
Bid/ask spreadThe gap between buyers' and sellers' prices — a hidden cost. Tight on big stocks, wide on small ones.
VolatilityNormal, not broken. Diversified stock portfolios historically drop 10% most years, 30%+ once a decade, and 50%+ occasionally — and recovered every time so far. Plan for it so you don't sell into it.
Valuation multiplesP/E (price ÷ earnings) and P/S (price ÷ sales) tell you how much future is already in the price. The quantum names on this hub at P/S 100–800× are priced for decades of perfection; a bank at P/E 8 is priced for trouble.
03 // INVESTING IN STOCKS
A Practical Path
STEP 1
Pick a regulated broker
Choose an established, regulated platform in your country. Compare: fees per trade, FX conversion cost (matters for US shares), account types (tax wrappers), and fractional shares. Avoid platforms that make money mainly when you trade more.
STEP 2
Core first — index funds
A global or S&P 500 index fund as your base gives instant diversification at ~0.03–0.2% fees. This is the "core" in core–satellite; most professionals fail to beat it.
STEP 3
Satellites — researched bets
Individual stocks and thematic sectors (this hub) sized so any single one can fail without hurting you — commonly 1–5% per position, 15–30% total. Write the thesis before buying.
STEP 4
Automate & ignore
Monthly auto-invest (dollar-cost averaging) removes timing decisions. Check quarterly, rebalance yearly, act on your written rules — not headlines.
READ THE NUMBERS
Minimum viable analysis
Before buying any stock know: what it sells, revenue growth, is it profitable (or path to it), debt level, and one number that proves the thesis (e.g. Symbotic's backlog). If you can't name that number, you're speculating, not investing.
PRACTICE
Paper trade first
Run positions in the Paper Portfolio for a season before using real money. Losing fake money teaches the same lessons at zero cost.
04 // ETFS EXPLAINED
The Most Useful Wrapper in Investing
QuestionAnswer
What is an ETF?A fund holding many assets that trades like a single share. One purchase = instant basket. Most track an index automatically.
Index vs thematicIndex ETFs (S&P 500, world) are cheap core holdings. Thematic ETFs (robotics, uranium, AI) are concentrated sector bets with 5–20× higher fees — satellite material, not core. See ETF Compare.
What to checkExpense ratio (annual fee), AUM (funds under ~$50M risk closure), top-10 concentration, and overlap with what you already own — three AI-ish ETFs often means triple-owning NVDA.
Accumulating vs distributingAccumulating reinvests dividends automatically (simpler, often better in taxable accounts depending on country); distributing pays them out as cash.
Hidden gotchasSynthetic replication, currency-hedged variants, and leveraged/inverse ETFs (avoid — they decay and are for day-traders, not investors).
05 // CRYPTO
Investing & Trading Digital Assets — With Both Eyes Open
SIZE IT HONESTLY
A small, deliberate allocation
Institutional research (BlackRock, Fidelity) supports 1–5% of a portfolio in bitcoin as an asymmetric diversifier. Expect 50%+ drawdowns as routine — size so that doesn't change your life or your sleep.
BTC ≠ EVERYTHING ELSE
Know the risk tiers
Bitcoin has an institutional adoption story and ETFs. Ethereum has a technology-platform story. Beyond those, risk rises steeply — most altcoins go to ~zero across cycles, and memecoins are lottery tickets by design.
HOW TO BUY
Three routes, different trade-offs
1) Bitcoin/crypto ETFs in your broker — simplest, taxed like shares, no custody burden. 2) Regulated exchanges — actual coins, withdrawal freedom. 3) Self-custody hardware wallet — full sovereignty, full responsibility (lose the seed phrase, lose everything).
SECURITY
The #1 practical risk is you
Use a unique password + hardware 2FA on exchanges, never store seed phrases digitally, assume every DM/"support agent"/too-good yield offer is a scam. "Not your keys, not your coins" — but also: your keys, your single point of failure.
TRADING REALITY
24/7 markets cut both ways
Crypto never closes, moves violently on leverage cascades, and is dominated by professionals and bots. Avoid leverage entirely as a beginner — liquidation math means being right eventually doesn't matter if you're wrong first.
TAX
Crypto is usually taxable
Most countries tax crypto disposals — including swapping coin-to-coin, not just cashing out. Keep records from day one; exchanges report to tax authorities in more countries every year.
06 // RISK MANAGEMENT
Investing vs Trading — and Staying Alive in Both
RuleWhy
Know which game you're playingInvesting = owning businesses for years; trading = renting price movement for days. Different skills, timeframes, and tax treatment. Most wealth is built by the first; most accounts are blown up attempting the second.
Position sizing beats predictionCap any single speculative position (1–5%) so being wrong is survivable. The pros' edge isn't better predictions — it's never taking a fatal loss.
Define the exit before entryWrite down what would prove you wrong (thesis broken, -X% stop, catalyst passed). Deciding while losing money is deciding badly. Use the Decision Journal.
Never use money with a deadlineRent, house deposits, and next year's tuition don't belong in markets. Forced selling at the bottom is how permanent losses happen.
Beware the crowd at extremesMaximum excitement = maximum priced-in. The hub's Risk Gauge exists because hot narratives (quantum, humanoids) carry the most air underneath.
Keep a cash sleeve5–10% cash converts crashes from emergencies into opportunities.
07 // COMMON MISTAKES
The Expensive Lessons, Pre-Paid
Chasing performance
Buying whatever went up most last year is the single most reliable way to underperform. Last year's winner is usually this year's most-expensive asset.
Confusing a story with a price
"AI will change the world" can be true while AI stocks lose you money — if you overpaid. The story and the entry price are separate questions.
Overtrading
Every trade costs spread + fees + tax + the chance of being wrong twice (selling and re-entering). Activity feels like control; it's usually just cost.
No written plan
Without pre-committed rules, you'll buy excitement and sell fear like everyone else. The plan is the edge.
All-in on one thing
Concentration builds fortunes in stories you hear about, and destroys them in the far more numerous stories you don't.
Ignoring FX and fees
A 1.5% currency conversion fee on every US trade, or a 1% annual fund fee, silently removes a quarter of a lifetime's gains.
