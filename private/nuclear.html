04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
First criticality still isn't commercial power
Oklo's Groves reactor achieving first criticality (Aug 2026) is real progress, but it's a small isotope test reactor, not a grid-connected commercial SMR. Zero advanced SMRs deliver commercial power anywhere; Clinch River — the furthest-along utility-scale application — still needs its Aug 13 mandatory hearing before a construction permit even issues, let alone gets built. First-of-a-kind builds historically run over budget and schedule (see Vogtle).
A founding backer stepping back is a bigger tell than a press release
Fluor has signaled plans to exit its NuScale stake in 2026 even as ENTRA1/TVA tout a non-binding 6 GW pathway and NuScale has already advanced a $495M milestone payment. When an early investor with inside knowledge of the technology reduces exposure, that carries more information than another non-binding MOU.
Regulatory clearance still isn't construction, and construction still isn't power
NRC's licensing-modernization rule (published Jul 16) remains in its comment period. TerraPower's Kemmerer permit was a genuine milestone — the first US approval of a commercial non-light-water reactor in 40+ years — yet the plant still won't generate power until roughly 2031. Multi-year gaps between permit and first power remain the norm across every design in this sector.
Valuations are still pricing 2030s outcomes today
Uranium spot ($86.48/lb) is well off the >$100 highs seen earlier this year despite term prices hitting a decade-high $94/lb. Pre-revenue names remain deeply loss-making — Oklo posted a $48.5M net loss on $1.2M of revenue, X-energy's Q1 loss deepened to $166.2M on $43.4M of revenue and grants — while trading on valuations that assume commercial success that hasn't happened yet.
