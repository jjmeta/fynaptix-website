04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Nothing is operating yet
Even with NRC staff now recommending the Clinch River construction permit and Oklo holding DOE startup authorization for Groves, zero advanced SMRs generate commercial power. Reactor Pilot Program entrants (Antares Nuclear, Valar Atomics, Aalo Atomics) hit criticality around the Jul 4 EO deadline, but those are small demonstration units, not utility-scale, grid-connected SMRs. First-of-a-kind builds historically run over budget and schedule (see Vogtle); the TVA/ENTRA1 6 GW NuScale deal remains non-binding pending a PPA.
Regulatory clearance still isn't construction
Clinch River's construction permit — the furthest-along SMR application — still needs the Aug 13 mandatory hearing before issuance, and a permit only starts construction, not operation. NRC's licensing-modernization rule (published Jul 16) is still in its comment period. Multi-year gaps between permit and first power remain the norm.
Commodity strength isn't translating to equity performance
Cameco's Q2 2026 report (Jul 31) showed uranium prices up 17% YoY and long-term contracts at decade highs, yet the company missed consensus earnings and revenue estimates. Gas turbines and renewables + storage remain faster to deploy for data centers, and SMR economics for the 2030s are still unproven.
Pre-earnings de-risking is hitting the developers hardest
OKLO fell to a 52-week-low zone (~$38-43) heading into its Aug 7 Q2 print, well off its post-authorization highs near $55, despite no negative company-specific news — investors are demanding execution proof (fuel loading, first criticality, binding PPAs) rather than pricing in further regulatory wins. X-energy and Centrus face similar scrutiny at their Aug 6 and Aug 13 reports.
