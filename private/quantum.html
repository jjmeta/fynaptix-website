04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Years from commercial advantage
Most systems remain R&D-phase; revenue today is mostly government contracts and research access, not commercial workloads.
Valuations price in perfection
Even after a 30–39% July drawdown, the pure-play trio rallied back toward multi-week highs into and after Q2 earnings (Aug 5–6) and has held those gains since — despite D-Wave missing on revenue/EPS and Rigetti's GAAP loss widening to $52.6M. P/S multiples of ~55–495× still price in years of flawless execution.
Insiders aren't buying
Insiders at IonQ, Rigetti and D-Wave have collectively sold ~$988M in stock over five years versus roughly $4.3M in insider purchases combined (as of Jul 22, 2026) — a persistent gap between executive selling and executive conviction.
Dilution as a business model
Pure-plays fund R&D via repeated equity raises; share counts grow relentlessly. CHIPS Act awards to Rigetti, D-Wave and others are structured partly as government equity stakes — another layer of dilution on top. Quantinuum's own first post-IPO quarter posted a $597M GAAP net loss against $8M revenue — a reminder that a large IPO cushion buys time, not immunity from burn.
Winner may not be listed
IBM/Google/Microsoft or private PsiQuantum could reach fault tolerance first, stranding pure-play premiums. Quantinuum is now listed (Nasdaq: QNT) but its economics remain closer to a pre-revenue R&D story than a commercial one.
