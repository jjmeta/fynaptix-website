04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Years from commercial advantage
Most systems remain R&D-phase; revenue today is mostly government contracts and research access, not commercial workloads.
Valuations price in perfection — and swing hard on macro, not fundamentals
The pure-play trio round-tripped a full selloff-and-recovery cycle again this stretch: a Sept 1-2 slide on jumping Treasury yields mostly reversed by Sept 4. P/S multiples of ~64-497× still price in years of flawless execution, and that kind of round-trip on macro noise alone remains a feature of the trade, not a bug.
Insiders aren't buying — and leadership churn plus litigation add new wrinkles
Insiders at IonQ, Rigetti and D-Wave have collectively sold a net of roughly $863M in stock over the trailing three years, versus minimal insider buying. D-Wave's insider sales specifically spiked to $36M in Q2 (from under $2M in Q1) right as its CFO transition became effective (Sept 2) and a securities-litigation investigation into the company's Q2 miss and CFO exit widened from one law firm to at least four.
Dilution as a business model
Pure-plays fund R&D via repeated equity raises; share counts grow relentlessly. CHIPS Act awards to Rigetti, D-Wave and others are structured partly as government equity stakes — another layer of dilution on top. Quantinuum's own first post-IPO quarter posted a $597M GAAP net loss against $8M revenue, and newly listed Pasqal has now fallen below its own SPAC reference price just eight trading days after a ~100× revenue multiple debut — a reminder that new entrants are still being priced on story, not statements.
Winner may not be listed
IBM/Google/Microsoft or private PsiQuantum could reach fault tolerance first, stranding pure-play premiums. IBM's Nighthawk r2 release (Sep 3, ahead of its own roadmap) and PsiQuantum's new Brookhaven National Lab partnership are fresh reminders that the hedged giants and well-capitalized private players can simply out-execute rather than race the pure-plays on public markets.
