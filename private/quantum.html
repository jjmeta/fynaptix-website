04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Years from commercial advantage
Most systems remain R&D-phase; revenue today is mostly government contracts and research access, not commercial workloads.
Valuations price in perfection — and swing hard on macro, not fundamentals
The Aug 21 rebound (+7-11.5%) was fully erased by Aug 28 (IonQ -7.7%, Rigetti -5.2%, D-Wave -5.1% in a single session), driven again by rising Treasury yields (the 30-year hit a 19-year high; the 10-year sat near 4.68%) rather than company news. P/S multiples of ~63–509× still price in years of flawless execution, and that kind of round-trip on macro noise alone is a feature of the trade, not a bug.
Insiders aren't buying — and leadership churn adds a new wrinkle
Insiders at IonQ, Rigetti and D-Wave have collectively sold a net of roughly $863M in stock over the trailing three years, versus minimal insider buying — a persistent gap between executive selling and executive conviction. D-Wave's longtime CFO John Markovich announced his retirement Aug 25 (effective Sept 2); the company says it's unrelated to any business or reporting dispute, but the timing lands right as the bookings-to-revenue conversion story becomes the central question for the stock.
Dilution as a business model
Pure-plays fund R&D via repeated equity raises; share counts grow relentlessly. CHIPS Act awards to Rigetti, D-Wave and others are structured partly as government equity stakes — another layer of dilution on top. Quantinuum's own first post-IPO quarter posted a $597M GAAP net loss against $8M revenue, and newly listed Pasqal's ~100× revenue multiple on debut suggests the market is still pricing new entrants on story, not statements.
Winner may not be listed
IBM/Google/Microsoft or private PsiQuantum could reach fault tolerance first, stranding pure-play premiums. IBM's HRL Laboratories acquisition (Aug 26) is another reminder that the hedged giants can simply buy capability rather than build or bet on it — a structural advantage the pure-plays don't have.
