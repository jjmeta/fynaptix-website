04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Years from commercial advantage
Most systems remain R&D-phase; revenue today is mostly government contracts, research access and early pilot deployments rather than broad commercial workloads. D-Wave's own Q2 revenue of just $3.08M — a 24-25% miss versus consensus — is a reminder that even companies with real production wins (like the NTT DOCOMO deployment) are still working off a tiny base.
A rally doesn't erase execution risk
Every major pure-play and private-market name rose this week on a wave of CHIPS Act and NVIDIA-platform news, but D-Wave's stock still climbed despite a 24-25% revenue miss and widening securities-litigation scrutiny, and Rigetti's DARPA QBI exclusion was formally confirmed the same week its shares rose 5.4% on sector sentiment alone. Sector-wide enthusiasm can paper over company-specific problems for a while, not indefinitely.
Insiders aren't buying — and the number keeps growing
Insiders at IonQ, Rigetti and D-Wave have now sold a net of roughly $988M in stock over the trailing five years (up from the ~$863M/three-year figure cited a month ago), with virtually no offsetting insider buying even as fresh government CHIPS equity stakes pile a new class of shareholder on top of an already dilution-heavy funding model.
Dilution as a business model
Pure-plays continue to fund R&D via repeated equity raises and government equity stakes; share counts grow relentlessly. Quantinuum's post-IPO financials show an even wider gap between $28-32M FY26 guidance and its now ~$14.3B market cap after this week's 10.7% rally, and Pasqal remains stuck near $7.94 — still roughly 60% below its debut-day close three weeks in — a reminder that new entrants are still priced on story, not statements.
Winner may not be listed — or may be a giant's subsidiary
IBM's Anderon just landed the largest quantum CHIPS award to date ($1B, matched by another $1B from IBM) for a wafer foundry that is neither a pure-play stock nor a venture-backed startup — it's a wholly-owned IBM subsidiary. Combined with PsiQuantum's and QuEra's continued private-market progress, this week reinforced that a meaningful share of the technology roadmap sits outside anything an investor can buy directly as a pure-play.

FIPS 140-2 sunset takes effect
NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program moved all remaining FIPS 140-2 validated certificates to the Historical list; only FIPS 140-3 validated implementations are now accepted for new federal procurement, a concrete step in the post-quantum cryptography migration mandated by June's executive order.

D-Wave's Q2 revenue miss draws fresh legal scrutiny
Q2 revenue of $3.08M missed consensus of $4.03-4.08M by roughly 24-25%, adding to the securities-litigation investigation that already spans four law firms, even as BMO Capital initiated coverage at Outperform the same week.

IonQ and Synopsys win Best Paper at IEEE Quantum Week
Their joint demonstration of quantum-accelerated computer-aided engineering workflows cut execution time up to 14.6% and took 1st place Best Paper — one of four such awards IonQ collected at the conference.
SEP 16, 2026
IBM's Anderon finalizes $1B CHIPS Act quantum foundry award
The largest single quantum CHIPS award to date, funding expansion of Anderon's 300mm pure-play quantum wafer foundry in Albany, NY, matched by another $1B in IBM cash, IP, assets and workforce.

NVIDIA launches CUDA-Q Logical for fault-tolerant computing
The new orchestration layer let Fermilab cut fault-tolerant architecture development from five months to three weeks (a 7x speedup) and now incorporates Sandia's QUOPS benchmark for utility-scale readiness.

Quantum Machines used NVQLink to route quantum and classical tasks across live qubits, GPUs and CPUs in a single program, exchanging measurements and control decisions in microseconds — the real-time responsiveness needed for quantum error correction.
