04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Public-market valuations still stretched, even after this week's rally
Palantir's climb to ~$177-178 on UBS's fresh $250 target keeps the stock well above 100x trailing earnings, and Michael Burry's unretracted case that the market cap could eventually fall below $100B hasn't gone away; the S&P Aerospace & Defense sub-index trades around 32x forward earnings versus roughly 20x for the broader S&P 500 — a premium the sector has to keep earning, not just borrowing against sentiment.
Private-market valuations are drawing bubble comparisons of their own
Early-stage defense-tech rounds are now pricing at 17x-50x revenue, and Anduril's targeted valuation — last discussed near $100B, with prediction markets now floating $112.5B by Sep 30 — remains unclosed roughly nine weeks after first being reported; the Bank for International Settlements has begun comparing the sector's forward-looking, scarcity-driven pricing to the AI-infrastructure buildout it has already flagged as bubble-like.
The Pentagon's own drone competitions are favoring private newcomers over listed pure-plays
Sep 18's Gauntlet II results put Neros, Perennial Autonomy, ORQA, XTEND and ModalAI atop the leaderboard, while Red Cat's Teal Drones, Ondas's Ascent Aerosystems and Kratos — all three publicly listed — missed the top five in their categories; a program built explicitly to surface the best autonomy vendors isn't currently surfacing the ones investors can buy on a US exchange.
Golden Dome's funding structure means a stopgap doesn't freeze it — it zeroes it
98% of Golden Dome's requested $17.5B FY27 funding needs a reconciliation bill, not the base defense budget, and a continuing resolution locks agencies at prior-year spending — which for Golden Dome is nothing, since the program has no FY26 baseline to revert to; Gen. Guetlein's Aug 12 warning has only hardened as September reporting describes the program's near-term progress as being at real risk.
Leadership and governance disruption at the primes hasn't fully resolved
L3Harris now has both a new CEO (Sam Mehta) and a new board chair (Lewis Hay) following Chris Kubasik's conduct-related ouster, and the stock remains 35% off its all-time high even as new contract wins (the $4.7B Lockheed Martin deal, VAMPIRE/APKWS combat use in Ukraine) suggest the underlying business is intact; the market hasn't yet decided whether this is a clean transition or an unfinished story.
AeroVironment's legal overhang survives even a run of good news
The $464.8M Army laser contract and the Sep 9 earnings beat have driven a real recovery, but the securities-fraud class actions tied to the SCAR/BADGER restatement are still working through the courts; neither a laser contract nor a strong quarter retroactively resolves the disclosure issue that triggered the litigation.
