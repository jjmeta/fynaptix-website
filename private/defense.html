04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Public-market valuations still elevated, in both directions
Palantir trades near 143x trailing earnings even after pulling back from its early-September highs, with Burry's unretracted case that the market cap could eventually fall below $100B still hanging over the stock; the S&P Aerospace & Defense sub-index trades around 32x forward earnings versus roughly 20x for the broader S&P 500. Rheinmetall shows the same dynamic can cut the other way — down ~36% YTD despite a record order book — proof that sentiment, not just fundamentals, is setting these prices.
AeroVironment's legal overhang survives even a blowout quarter
The Sep 9 beat — record $480.5M revenue, $0.59 EPS, a $1.5B backlog — sent shares up 10%, but the securities-fraud class actions tied to January's SCAR/BADGER stop-work order and the resulting $87.3M restatement error are still proceeding through the courts; a strong print doesn't retroactively fix the disclosure issue that triggered the litigation.
The defense-tech VC boom still looks like a bubble
Anduril's targeted $100B round remains unclosed nearly eight weeks after first being reported, and CEO Brian Schimpf has said outright he isn't sure the market is pricing the sector rationally — even as Castelion closed a $1B round at a $13B valuation on Aug 20 and Saronic added another $55M in early September, showing capital is still chasing the space at a pace some insiders themselves call unsustainable.
December's funding cliff is still just a cliff, not a resolution
The Sep 3 stopgap funds government (and defense) through Dec 11 at FY26 levels, but Congress has passed only 3 of 12 full-year spending bills in the House and none in the Senate; the $54.6B DAWG autonomy line item, the CCA Increment 2 downselect, and Golden Dome's reconciliation-dependent funding all remain unresolved with the clock reset rather than stopped.
Governance and program-execution risk hits even established primes
L3Harris's Aug 17 ouster of CEO Chris Kubasik for conduct "inconsistent with company values" still hasn't been fully explained a month later, and JPMorgan's Sep 10 Negative Catalyst Watch on Rheinmetall landed even as the company posted a record order book — a reminder that backlogs don't insulate a prime from sudden leadership or sentiment shocks.
