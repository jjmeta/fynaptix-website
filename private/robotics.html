02 // SUB-SECTOR MAP
Where the Money Is Moving
HUMANOID
General-purpose humanoids
Unitree's post-IPO crash is now confirmed as a two-week slide: Bloomberg's Sep 2 report has shares down ~50% from their Aug 19 intraday peak, a "fast reversal" that has hardened bubble warnings (Chatham House, BIS) rather than settling them. On the demand side, BMW's next-gen Figure 03 has replaced the retired Figure 02 at Spartanburg (which built 30,000+ X3s) and BMW is now piloting humanoids at its Leipzig, Germany plant too — real, if narrow, proof of deployed autonomy. Tesla's Optimus remains unverified — Musk's July shareholder letter still describes Fremont lines "being installed" with output "anticipated later this year."
WAREHOUSE
Warehouse & logistics automation
Symbotic's story remains constructive: shares held near $42.81 (Sep 6, ~$26B market cap) after Q3 FY26 revenue hit $721M (+22% YoY) with GAAP profitability reached and backlog near $22.5-22.7B — a real improvement from the prior Goldman price-target cut and CTO share sale, though Barclays' Sell rating and the recent dilutive offering haven't gone away.
SURGICAL
Surgical robotics
Intuitive Surgical traded near $366 (Sep 6), still down roughly a third YTD and on pace for its weakest year since 2008 — but Oppenheimer upgraded the stock to Outperform this week with a $500 target (24%+ implied upside), and the broader analyst consensus target ($508.68) points the same way. J&J's Ottava added a fresh data point: its bariatric-surgery pivotal study met endpoints at the ASMBS meeting, building on its July FDA de novo authorization. Medtronic's Hugo remains live in 30+ countries on its urology clearance.
DEFENSE
Defense autonomy & drones
AeroVironment's $464.8M US Army LOCUST X3 laser-system award (Sep 3) sent shares up ~6%, but AVAV is still down roughly 40% YTD heading into its Sep 9 earnings report. Kratos picked up a new $20M+ contract from an Asian defense customer for mobile satcom gateways (Sep 1) and carries a $2.08B backlog, yet KTOS is still down 37% YTD. Contract wins and share prices remain decoupled in this sub-sector.
AG + CONSTRUCTION
Field robotics
Caterpillar's new partnership with FieldAI (Sep 2) — its first concrete physical-AI tie-up this year — pairs Cat's jobsite data and engineering expertise with FieldAI's robot-agnostic foundation models for autonomous inspections, digital twins and situational awareness. Deere's autonomous articulated dump truck and commercial mowers continue routine operation. Both remain diversified industrials where robotics is a small slice of the overall return.
JAPAN FA
Japanese factory automation
Fanuc has slipped further, trading near ¥5,725 (Sep 2) versus ¥6,063 the prior week — confirmation the recovery from the Aug 3 guidance shock has stalled rather than resumed, even against a ¥7,351 average target; shares go ex-dividend Sep 29. Yaskawa has pulled back to the ¥4,500 area (Sep 3) after last week's rally. Keyence remains the outlier, trading near record highs in the ¥70,000-72,600 range.
COMPONENTS
Supply chain & components
MP Materials rallied to $54.53 (Sep 5) after Q2 revenue of $108.49M beat estimates by 13% on a 127% surge in NdPr sales volumes and adjusted EBITDA swung $41M higher YoY — then jumped further Sep 4 on reports Chinese producers are pausing US rare-earth shipments, with William Blair adding MP to its September Conviction List. Vishay Precision Group remains 2026's best-performing robotics supply-chain stock on booked humanoid-actuator orders.
AI COMPUTE
The robot "brain"
NVIDIA's Jetson Thor platform (Blackwell-powered, 7.5x the AI compute of Jetson Orin) reached general availability in August, and the company unveiled new Blackwell-powered T3000/T2000 modules this week aimed at pushing mass-market robotics and edge AI further downmarket. Analog Devices became the latest Jetson Thor adopter (Sep 3), joining Agility, Amazon Robotics, Boston Dynamics, Caterpillar, Figure, Hexagon, Medtronic and Meta on a platform now used by 2M+ developers. The Aug 26 Q2 FY27 report (revenue $96.22B, Data Center $89B, Q3 guide $108B) still shows the compute layer dominating the sector's growth.
CHINA
China scaling & listing
Unitree's extended slump is compounded by the FCC's new ban on foreign-made humanoid/quadruped robot imports, a direct regulatory headwind given China's ~85% share of the global humanoid market. LimX Dynamics closed a $200M pre-IPO round (valuing it at $2.2B / RMB 15B) and is preparing a Hong Kong listing; Agibot continues scaling shipments. Chinese humanoid startups are increasingly racing toward IPOs partly to open funding channels the FCC action and US investment curbs have narrowed.

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Unitree's crash is the bubble case made real
Bloomberg reported Sep 2 that shares are now down ~50% from their Aug 19 intraday peak, a "fast reversal" now in its second straight week. Chatham House published a piece this month asking outright whether China's humanoid industry is a bubble, and the BIS has separately flagged that AI-linked asset valuations look overextended. This is no longer a hypothetical bubble warning — it's a live repricing investors can watch happen, now compounded by the FCC's new ban on foreign humanoid imports.
Humanoid funding still outruns humanoid revenue
Dealroom puts 2026 robotics funding near $55.8B — nearly double last year's record — while the median disclosed humanoid round remains in the hundreds of millions with rounds above $50M now the overwhelming majority of capital raised. Most of these companies still don't have revenue that justifies the price tag; one widely cited bear case on Figure AI specifically flags its valuation against Goldman's 2035 humanoid TAM estimate and an ongoing whistleblower lawsuit over safety allegations — notably in tension with BMW's own announcement this year expanding Figure deployments to a second plant in Leipzig, a discrepancy worth watching for clarification.
Demos ≠ autonomy
If a humanoid demo isn't explicitly labeled autonomous, assume teleoperation. Dexterity, inference speed, reliability, and cost still confine real use to structured factory/warehouse tasks.
Crowding & shakeout risk
150+ companies chasing humanoids, with Agibot alone now claiming 15,000 cumulative units shipped; a reshuffling of startups is widely expected. Robotics pioneer Rodney Brooks argues much of the investment will be wasted.
Backlogs and beats don't protect the stock
Symbotic got a fresh Goldman price-target cut (to $45 from $54) and an insider share sale this week — on top of last week's dilutive offering — despite real revenue growth. AeroVironment and Kratos both won new contracts this month, yet their shares have traded down in 2026 anyway. In parts of this sector, balance-sheet and sentiment signals are doing more work than headline numbers.
Revenue is elsewhere
Industrial, logistics, and surgical robots generate proven revenue today; humanoids largely don't yet. Component and warehouse names carry less thesis risk than humanoid pure-plays.
China price war
Unitree's $5,900 R1 signals aggressive price compression that could squeeze Western OEM margins before the market matures — and 150+ Chinese humanoid startups, now including BYD, are competing on price and scale even as Unitree's own stock struggles to hold its valuation.
"Dot-com stage" warnings
KB Securities likened 2026's robotics/space enthusiasm to the final stage of the dot-com bubble; Ray Dalio separately compared the broader AI bubble to 1929 and 2000 valuation extremes. Sector EV/EBITDA multiples ranged from 0.9x (mature industrials) to 78.2x (speculative pure-plays) in Q4 2025 — a valuation split investors should not treat as one trade.
