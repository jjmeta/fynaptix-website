02 // SUB-SECTOR MAP
Where the Money Is Moving
HUMANOID
General-purpose humanoids
Unitree's Shanghai STAR Market debut (Aug 19, ticker 688836) opened up 629% before paring gains to close +460% at roughly RMB 342B (~$50B) — briefly touching an implied ~$66B intraday — putting the profitable, fast-growing humanoid maker at a reported ~857x 2026 earnings versus the STAR Market's ~130x average. BYD confirmed its first humanoid, "Xiao Di" (1.61m, 58.5kg, real-time translation across six languages), for its Di Space showrooms this month, targeting deployment across 100+ scenarios by year-end. Tesla's Optimus remains unverified: Musk now says output will be "quite slow" given 10,000 unique new parts, with early units routed to an internal "Optimus Academy" training program rather than customers.
WAREHOUSE
Warehouse & logistics automation
Symbotic's momentum reversed hard this week: shares fell roughly 25% after a surprise SEC filing disclosed a share offering (6.5M Class A shares plus 3.5M more from a SoftBank-controlled holder), and Barclays reaffirmed its Sell rating even after last week's beat-and-raise Q3 FY26 print ($721M revenue, $55M GAAP net income). The stock is now at a fresh 2026 low, down roughly a third for the year — a reminder that a strong quarter doesn't protect against dilution.
SURGICAL
Surgical robotics
Intuitive Surgical fell 5.8% on Aug 20 as a cautious outlook overshadowed an Aug 12 Oppenheimer upgrade to Outperform; shares are down roughly 30% YTD, on pace for the weakest year since 2008, even as procedure growth (+16%) and revenue growth (+18-19%) stay strong and da Vinci 5 just won FDA clearance for cardiac procedures. The competitive backdrop got more concrete too: J&J's Ottava now has FDA de novo authorization for a full slate of soft-tissue procedures — no longer just a threat on paper.
DEFENSE
Defense autonomy & drones
The Trump administration's new drone tariffs took effect Aug 14 — 100% on unmanned systems over 25kg or with "sensitive" features like thermal imaging, 25% on smaller commercial/hobbyist drones — handing AVAV and KTOS a durable US-market pricing edge on top of Kratos's new Army Javelin seeker-upgrade contract and potential $156M DOE Solar Shield award. Pentagon FY27 budget still requests ~$75B for unmanned/counter-drone systems.
AG + CONSTRUCTION
Field robotics
Deere's autonomous articulated dump truck continues quarry operations under its 2nd-gen autonomy kit; Caterpillar's five-machine autonomous construction lineup remains in early rollout. Both stay diversified industrials where robotics is a small slice of the overall return — no fresh sector-specific news this week.
JAPAN FA
Japanese factory automation
Keyence hit a fresh limit-up this week on continued double-digit revenue and profit growth, with Fanuc (holding near ¥6,720, 9-buy/1-sell) and Yaskawa (+¥312 to ¥5,462) rallying alongside it as the sector works through the aftermath of Fanuc's Aug 3 guidance shock. Still too early to call it a re-rating rather than a bounce.
COMPONENTS
Supply chain & components
Harmonic Drive Systems lifted its full-year guidance Aug 7 on strong automation demand, then struck a more cautious note in its Aug 19 Q1 FY2027 report. HSBC downgraded Cognex to Hold from Buy and cut its price target to $65 from $94 this week, even as the company's new $0.085 dividend went ex-date Aug 20. MP Materials rose 16.4% over the week on a defense offtake deal and Canaccord roadmap commentary, despite still posting a GAAP net loss.
AI COMPUTE
The robot "brain"
NVIDIA's Jetson Thor platform keeps widening its lead as the default robot brain — Chinese robotics firm Galbot became the latest adopter this month, integrating Jetson AGX Thor into its G1 Premium humanoid alongside existing users spanning Agility, Boston Dynamics, Figure and Caterpillar. Physical Intelligence is reportedly in talks for a new ~$1B round at a valuation north of $11B — nearly double the $5.6B mark from its round just four months earlier — sharpening the software-layer race with Skild AI's "Skild Brain 1.0."
CHINA

05 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Bubble warnings from serious sources
China's NDRC has warned of a humanoid "bubble"; Morgan Stanley flags a wide gap between "dancing robots" and robots with scalable practical value. Global humanoid shipments reached roughly 19,000-22,000 units in H1 2026 (+272-300% YoY per Counterpoint) — real growth, but still a fraction of the volume implied by the sector's funding and valuation levels.
Marks — public or private — don't always hold
Unitree's own debut showed how fast a mark can move: shares opened +629% before paring to +460% by the close, an implied ~857x 2026 earnings versus the STAR Market's ~130x average. Figure's Forge secondary prices already imply a softer number than its $39B primary round. A real trade can correct a mark overnight, in either direction.
Demos ≠ autonomy
If a humanoid demo isn't explicitly labeled autonomous, assume teleoperation. Dexterity, inference speed, reliability, and cost still confine real use to structured factory/warehouse tasks.
Crowding & shakeout risk
150+ companies chasing humanoids; a reshuffling of startups is widely expected. Robotics pioneer Rodney Brooks argues much of the investment will be wasted.
A beat doesn't protect the stock
Symbotic beat and raised in its Q3 FY26 print, then fell ~25% in a week anyway after a surprise dilutive share offering and a reaffirmed Barclays Sell rating. Intuitive Surgical dropped 5.8% on a cautious outlook despite an analyst upgrade days earlier. In parts of this sector, balance-sheet and guidance signals are doing more work than the headline earnings number.
Revenue is elsewhere
Industrial, logistics, and surgical robots generate proven revenue today; humanoids largely don't yet. Component and warehouse names carry less thesis risk than humanoid pure-plays.
China price war
Unitree's $5,900 R1 signals aggressive price compression that could squeeze Western OEM margins before the market matures — and 150+ Chinese humanoid startups, now including BYD, are competing on price and scale.
"Dot-com stage" warnings
KB Securities likened 2026's robotics/space enthusiasm to the final stage of the dot-com bubble; Ray Dalio separately compared the broader AI bubble to 1929 and 2000 valuation extremes. Sector EV/EBITDA multiples ranged from 0.9x (mature industrials) to 78.2x (speculative pure-plays) in Q4 2025 — a valuation split investors should not treat as one trade.
