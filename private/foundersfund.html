01 // THE THESIS
What Founders Fund Actually Believes
FOUNDING BELIEF
Competition is for losers
Peter Thiel's most contrarian thesis: genuinely great companies don't compete — they create monopolies. Founders Fund avoids investment themes that are hot or consensus because by the time everyone is excited, the easy money has been made.
THE ESSAY
"We Wanted Flying Cars, Instead We Got 140 Characters"
Founders Fund's founding manifesto argued that VC had become incrementalist — billions into social apps while genuinely hard problems (space, disease, energy) went unfunded. The fund exists as a rebuke to that trend.
HARD SCIENCE BIAS
Atoms, not just bits
FF explicitly prioritises companies working on physical reality — rockets, weapons systems, genomics, energy, materials science. They believe software returns are becoming commoditised and the next enormous value will come from companies that marry deep science with the ability to build at scale.
GEOPOLITICAL LENS
America must maintain its technological edge
Long before national security tech was a VC category, FF backed Palantir for data analytics and later Anduril for autonomous defence. Their thesis: a technologically weak America is dangerous for the world, and private capital has a role in maintaining that edge.
CONTRARIAN TIMING
The best opportunities look wrong at the time
SpaceX seemed absurd in 2008. Palantir seemed paranoid. Anduril seemed controversial. Founders Fund has a higher tolerance for reputational risk than almost any other major VC. The common thread: the thing only works if something most people think impossible turns out to be possible.
CONCENTRATION
Big bets, not broad diversification
Founders Fund runs concentrated positions in a small number of companies they believe will be transformative. The highest-conviction approach in venture — producing both the biggest wins and the biggest failures.

05 // HOW TO READ FOUNDERS FUND
Strengths, Blind Spots & the Honest Assessment
The counter-consensus calls are legitimately impressive
SpaceX. Palantir. Anduril. Facebook at seed. DeepMind before it was fashionable. FF's big wins aren't lucky — they reflect sustained willingness to back things that seemed impossible or inappropriate.
Their network effect in deep tech is real
Peter Thiel, through the PayPal Mafia, Palantir and Founders Fund, has created a denser network of deep-tech founders and operators than almost any other single investor.
The politics are part of the deal
Peter Thiel's political activities create reputational complexity for some founders and LPs. Some defence investments are explicitly political in framing. That's a feature for some, a bug for others — it's not separable from the fund.
Concentration cuts both ways
When SpaceX is your biggest position and working, returns are extraordinary. When Stemcentrx was acquired for $5.8B and then written down to zero within two years, the concentrated position hurt.
How to use Founders Fund research
Zero to One is worth reading as a framework — not investment advice but a lens for evaluating genuine competitive moat versus being in a growing market.
