03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells
In grid storage (ESS) specifically, CATL shipped 125.0 GWh in H1 2026 (+81% YoY) for a 27.1% overall share, with BYD fourth at 35.7 GWh (7.7% share); in EV batteries the two hold a combined 54.3% of global shipments. North America/Europe/other now account for 56% of ESS shipment destinations for the first time — geographic diversification is real, but manufacturing scale still sits overwhelmingly with Chinese cell makers.
Policy whiplash is already claiming casualties
Section 301 duties on non-EV lithium-ion (grid BESS) cells jumped from 7.5% to 25% effective Jan 1, 2026, pushing the effective combined US import tariff on Chinese ESS batteries to ~58.4%; new FEOC rules cap Chinese-sourced content at 45% of manufacturing cost for ITC eligibility. Wood Mackenzie now projects the US utility-scale storage market could contract 11–29% in 2026 on this policy uncertainty, and integrator Powin's Chapter 11 liquidation (assets sold to FlexGen) is a concrete example of a tariff-driven casualty.
Margin compression
Fluence's Q3 FY26 print is the freshest evidence: GAAP gross margin fell to 5.1% from 14.8% on contract-manufacturing delays, with $400M of deliveries now slipping into FY27 — on top of Tesla's own Q2 energy-segment margin nearly halving (39.5% → 20.4%) and Eos Energy's gross margin still near -69%. Software attach (Stem's 55% non-GAAP gross margin) remains the differentiator; hardware stays under real pressure from Chinese cost structures.
Solid-state timelines
QuantumScape's Eagle Line pilot is producing and the Honda pact holds, but there's still no production license or volume commitment; 2026 adjusted EBITDA-loss guidance remains $250–275M and shares are down more than 50% YTD, underscoring how far off meaningful revenue still is.
