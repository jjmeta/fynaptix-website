03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells
A fresh analysis this week found CATL's grid-storage (ESS) battery revenue actually fell 1% in H1 2026 even as shipment volumes kept growing, with the tariff impact on that gap still unclear — a reminder that Chinese cell makers are absorbing margin pressure to defend share rather than ceding it. CATL and BYD together still dominate global cell manufacturing scale; North America/Europe/other reaching 56% of ESS shipment destinations for the first time is geographic diversification, not a change in who makes the cells.
Policy whiplash is already claiming casualties
Wood Mackenzie now projects an 11% contraction in the US utility-scale storage market in 2026 and a further 8% decline in 2027 as FEOC content restrictions (in force since Jan 1, 2026) and Section 301 tariffs bottleneck FEOC-compliant equipment and safe-harbored capacity — even as the firm still expects the market to quadruple toward ~200GW/655GWh cumulative by 2031 once domestic manufacturing, like LG Energy Solution's newly opened 35GWh Lansing, MI plant, comes fully online. Over $32B of US clean-energy storage projects were already canceled or downsized in 2025, and integrator Powin's Chapter 11 liquidation remains the clearest casualty to date.
Margin compression
Eos Energy is this week's starkest evidence: a Q2 net loss that widened to $275.7M, a tightened FY26 revenue guide ($300–350M from $300–400M), and new securities-law-firm investigations opened Aug 20. Fluence's analyst base is fracturing in the same direction — GLJ downgraded to Hold and Citi trimmed its own target to $20 from $24 — even as the company's $6.4B backlog says demand isn't the constraint. Software attach (Stem's 55% non-GAAP gross margin) remains the differentiator; hardware stays under real pressure.
Solid-state timelines
QuantumScape's Cobra-process cells keep shipping to Volkswagen and appeared on the Ducati V21L, but an Aug 18 Scientific American analysis explicitly questioned whether QuantumScape and peers like Factorial Energy can mass-produce solid-state cells at all against Chinese-dominated manufacturing scale — there is still no production license, no volume commitment, and no revenue.
