03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells — and is now adding a new cost lever
CATL shipped 125GWh of storage cells in H1 2026 (27.1% of the global market) while BYD took the integrator crown from Tesla with 60GWh shipped in 2025 versus Tesla's 46.7GWh; the two are now underbidding each other abroad — BYD won an 11.275GWh Masdar (UAE) storage deal that was initially expected to go to CATL. CATL confirmed Aug 20 that its Naxtra sodium-ion cells will hit cost parity with LFP by end-2026, with first China deliveries of its TENER Sodium grid system starting September — a fresh cost-curve threat layered on top of existing scale dominance.
Policy whiplash is already claiming casualties
Wood Mackenzie's projected 11% contraction in the US utility-scale storage market in 2026 (with a further 8% decline in 2027) as FEOC content restrictions and Section 301 tariffs bottleneck compliant equipment still frames the risk, even as EIA's own numbers show capacity still climbing — nearly 52GW online, a record 24GW of 2026 additions planned, and a path to 64.9GW by year-end. Over $32B of US clean-energy storage projects were already canceled or downsized in 2025, and integrator Powin's Chapter 11 liquidation remains the clearest casualty to date; 2027's planned adds (26GW) look more like deceleration than the quadrupling bulls expect by 2031.
Margin compression
Fluence's Q3 revenue landed ~$90M below expectations on contract-manufacturer ramp delays, forcing a $400M cut (at the midpoint) to FY26 guidance even as backlog hit a record $6.4B — demand isn't the constraint, delivery is. Eos Energy's Aug 27 Thorn Hill consolidation targets a 10–15% conversion-cost cut, but not until 2027, and doesn't touch the Q2 net loss that widened to $275.7M or the open securities-law investigations. Stem beat on EPS but still had its price target cut on a soft revenue guide — margin discipline and top-line growth keep trading off against each other across the sector.
Solid-state timelines
QuantumScape's Cobra-process cells keep shipping to Volkswagen and appeared on the Ducati V21L, and Honda has now signed on for solid-state R&D and manufacturing collaboration, but Truist cut its price target to $6 from $8 this week and the Street's targets range from $2.50 to $10 — a sign of genuine disagreement about whether QuantumScape (or peers like Factorial Energy) can mass-produce solid-state cells at all against Chinese-dominated manufacturing scale. Still no production license, no volume commitment, and no revenue.
