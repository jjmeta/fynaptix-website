03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells — and domestic rivalry is compressing terms further
CATL still holds the global No.1 BESS shipment position for H1 2026 (storage now 19.23% of its revenue), and just locked in a 10-year, up-to-200GWh supply deal with Beijing HyperStrong running through 2028 — deepening its grip on the value chain. BYD and Sungrow's earlier win of the Masdar deal already showed domestic rivalry compresses terms on its own; combined, CATL and BYD now account for 62.43% of battery installations, the highest concentration in recent months.
Tariffs keep rising, and FEOC/FERC rules add layers of cost and complexity
Section 301 duties on Chinese-origin ESS batteries jumped from 40.9% in 2025 to 58.4% in 2026, and FEOC content thresholds (55% domestic in 2026, rising to 75% after 2029) still risk disqualifying projects from the ITC. Layered on top, the FERC-directed PJM co-location rule rewrite remains mid-implementation in 2026 — transmission cost allocation under the new framework directly affects grid-services revenue for data-center-adjacent storage names like NRGV and Cummins' customers.
Fluence's crisis is now weighing on how the market reads the whole hardware/software stack
Fluence cut FY26 revenue guidance by $600M and swung its EBITDA outlook from a $10M loss to a $200M loss, disclosed an SEC investigation into revenue recognition and internal controls, and saw shares fall 15.4% in a single session — on top of three sell-side downgrades already in place from early September. At least five law firms have since opened shareholder investigations. Stem's slide to a fresh 52-week low despite positive adjusted EBITDA suggests the market is now discounting execution risk across the software/optimization layer too, not just Fluence's hardware integration.
Solid-state timelines keep slipping while China's catches up
QuantumScape shares are down roughly 51% YTD with no fresh catalysts this month, and recent CTO share sales add a mildly negative signal; the PowerCo licensing deal still targets only a 2027 commercial launch with no production license or revenue yet. CATL and BYD are both still publicly targeting 2027 solid-state trial production, meaning the assumed Western first-mover advantage in solid-state keeps eroding rather than firming up.
