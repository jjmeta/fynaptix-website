03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells — and keeps adding cost levers
China's domestic power-battery installations hit 76.5GWh in June alone (+31.5% YoY), with CATL holding 42.7% share and BYD 18.5%; both are now exporting that scale advantage abroad — BYD's 11.275GWh Masdar (Abu Dhabi) storage win and a new CATL-Alfen supply deal bringing 5GWh of sodium-ion batteries to Europe show the pattern spreading beyond home turf. CATL's Naxtra sodium-ion cells remain on track for cost parity with LFP by year-end, with first China deliveries of the TENER Sodium grid system beginning this month — a cost-curve threat layered on top of existing scale dominance.
Tariffs and policy add a second axis of pressure
Section 301 tariffs on Chinese-origin battery energy storage systems rose to 25% on January 1, 2026, pushing the combined tariff burden on Chinese batteries and BESS to roughly 55%, while FEOC content thresholds now disqualify projects leaning too heavily on restricted foreign entities — starting at 55% in 2026 and tightening to 75% after 2029. Wood Mackenzie still projects an 11% contraction in the US utility-scale storage market in 2026 (and a further 8% decline in 2027) even as EIA data shows capacity still climbing toward 64.9GW by year-end — compliance costs are rising regardless of which growth trajectory proves right.
Margin compression
Barclays downgraded Fluence to Underweight on Sept 3 with a $10 price target, and shares fell to roughly $10.36 — a fresh sign that the ~$400M of Q3 deliveries pushed into fiscal 2027 on contract-manufacturer delays is a durable concern, not a one-quarter blip, even against a record $6.4B backlog. Eos Energy's Sept 2 Google/MN8 partnership is a genuine technology and logo win, but it generates no storage revenue until 2029–2030 and does nothing for the Q2 net loss that widened to $275.7M or the open securities-law investigations — the sector keeps proving it can win orders faster than it can convert them to margin.
Solid-state timelines
QuantumScape has gone quiet since its July Q2 print and June Honda R&D agreement — no September catalysts have emerged, and Truist's $6 price target (cut from $8 in August) still stands against a Street range of $2.50–$10. The absence of news is itself a data point: with no production license, no volume commitment, and no revenue, the gap between demonstrated lab performance and mass-manufacturable solid-state cells sits exactly where it was a month ago.
