03 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
China dominates cells
In global EV batteries, CATL (39.9%, 242.7 GWh, +25.3% YoY) and BYD (14.4%, 87.7 GWh) held a combined 54.3% of H1 2026 shipments; in grid storage specifically, global ESS shipments hit 461.3 GWh in H1 2026 (+71% YoY) with CATL alone holding a 35% share of that segment. North America/Europe/other now account for 56% of ESS shipment destinations (vs 43.9% China) for the first time — a sign of geographic diversification, but manufacturing scale still sits overwhelmingly with Chinese cell makers.
Policy whiplash
Section 301 duties on non-EV lithium-ion (grid BESS) cells jumped from 7.5% to 25% effective Jan 1, 2026, pushing the effective combined US import tariff on Chinese ESS batteries to ~58.4% (from 40.9%); new FEOC rules cap Chinese-sourced content at 45% of manufacturing cost for ITC eligibility.
Margin compression
Fluence's Q3 FY26 print is the freshest evidence: GAAP gross margin fell to 5.1% from 14.8% on contract-manufacturing delays and upfront battery-supply costs, forcing a guidance cut and a ~16% share-price drop — on top of Tesla's own Q2 energy-segment margin nearly halving (39.5% → 20.4%). Software attach remains the differentiator for integrators; hardware is under real pressure from Chinese cost structures.
Solid-state timelines
QuantumScape's Honda pact is a vote of confidence but still carries no production license or volume commitment; 2026 adjusted EBITDA-loss guidance remains $250–275M and shares are down more than 50% YTD, underscoring how far off meaningful revenue still is.
