03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
SUMMIT COUNTDOWN
Xi's Washington visit is now 10 days out
Xi Jinping is expected to arrive in Washington late Sep 23 and depart Sep 25 — an unusually compressed visit that skips the UN General Assembly entirely. Rare-earth licensing is explicitly on the agenda; Treasury Secretary Bessent has said the US expects Beijing to fully meet its rare-earth commitments. The visit lands 47 days before China's expanded export-control suspension expires Nov 10, and 99 days before tougher US defense sourcing rules take effect Jan 1, 2027.
SUPPLIERS STILL PULLING BACK
Chinese shipment halts remain unresolved heading into the summit
The Chinese rare-earth suppliers who quietly halted shipments to US buyers in early August — wary of Beijing penalties tied to Responsible Minerals Initiative compliance — have not resumed as of mid-September. Yttrium exports to the US remain roughly half their 2024 level. China separately placed MP Materials and USA Rare Earth on an export-control list back in June, a reminder the friction predates and could outlast this month's diplomacy.
TARIFF LIMBO
A copper tariff the market was pricing in may not arrive
Freeport fell 6.6% on Sep 11 on reports the White House still hasn't decided whether to proceed with a Section 232 tariff on refined copper cathodes phased in from 2027 (proposed at 15%, rising to 30% by 2028), after already imposing 50% tariffs on raw copper and derivative products in 2025-26. Losing that tailwind doesn't break the copper thesis, but it removes an upside catalyst the market had partly priced in.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
Lithium carbonate's global benchmark slipped 1.15% to $21.73/kg on Sep 9 amid an unexpected surplus in Chinese inventories — a reminder that 2026's price recovery is not a one-way trade. Copper's YoY gain has also decelerated, easing from roughly +47.6% two weeks ago to about +41.2% now, down nearly 2% over the trailing month. Both metals can still swing sharply on a single mine, policy, or inventory surprise.
China can flood the market — or freeze the tap
The same country whose inventory surplus is pressuring lithium carbonate prices is, per ongoing reporting, also the one whose suppliers are still refusing to ship rare earths to some US buyers over fear of Beijing sanctions. Investors betting on either a supply glut or a supply squeeze are making a bet on Beijing's posture, not just on markets — and that posture is exactly what's on the table at the Sep 24 summit.
Policy tailwinds aren't guaranteed to arrive
Freeport's 6.6% one-day drop on copper-tariff uncertainty shows how much of the sector's bull case rests on policy that hasn't actually been finalized. The same is true elsewhere: MP Materials' 10X plant EBITDA guarantee, Energy Fuels' $725M conditional government support, and Project Vault's $10B EXIM-backed stockpile are all real, but each carries execution and political risk between announcement and cash landing.
Leadership and integration overhangs persist
Albemarle's strike was averted but its new CEO doesn't take over until Feb 2027; Lynas' CEO search under interim chief Pol Le Roux has now run more than two months with no replacement named; and USA Rare Earth is still digesting 126.85M shares of dilution from the Serra Verde close while integrating assets across four countries. None of these is disqualifying on its own, but none is resolved either.
