03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
SUPPLIERS PULL BACK
Chinese suppliers quietly halt shipments ahead of Xi's visit
Reuters reported Sep 4 that a handful of Chinese rare-earth suppliers have refused to ship to US buyers since early August, wary of Beijing penalties for complying with the Responsible Minerals Initiative's due-diligence framework. Yttrium exports to the US remain at roughly half their 2024 level. The issue is now formally on Washington's agenda ahead of Xi Jinping's Sep 24 Washington visit.
SUMMIT COUNTDOWN
Three clocks now running — Sep 24, Nov 10, Nov 27
Xi's Sep 24 Washington visit is the nearest checkpoint, with rare-earth access explicitly on the agenda; Treasury Secretary Bessent has said the US expects Beijing to fully meet its rare-earth commitments. Behind it sit two truce deadlines — general rare-earth/battery-material export licenses run through roughly Nov 10, 2026, and the Busan truce's gallium/germanium/antimony/superhard pause runs through Nov 27, 2026 — while China's own exit-entry rules tighten from Sep 15 to curb outbound knowhow.
CONSOLIDATION
Onshoring goes from financing to combining
USA Rare Earth's Sep 3 close of its $2.8B Serra Verde combination is the clearest sign yet that Project Vault-era onshoring policy (a $10B EXIM loan plus $2B private capital, backing a 60-mineral strategic reserve) is translating into actual corporate consolidation, not just government financing announcements. The combined USAR-Serra Verde entity becomes one of the only fully integrated rare-earth-to-magnet platforms outside Asia, with Serra Verde bringing scaled heavy-rare-earth production USAR lacked on its own.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
China lithium carbonate spot sits around $23,161/t (Sep 3, +37% YTD) — up sharply on the year but essentially range-bound recently, with 2026 full-year forecasts revised up even as analysts expect H2 softening. A possible delay to CATL's Jianxiawo mine restart complicates the simple oversupply story. Copper has held up better (+47.6% YoY) but is still down slightly over the trailing month — a reminder that both metals can swing on a single mine or regulatory decision.
China can flood the market — or freeze the tap
The same country whose restarted mines feed lithium-glut fears is, per Reuters' Sep 4 reporting, also the one whose suppliers are voluntarily halting US rare-earth shipments over fear of Beijing sanctions tied to the Responsible Minerals Initiative. Investors betting on either a supply glut or a supply squeeze are making a bet on Beijing's posture, not just on markets.
Execution and leadership risk across the board
This week hit nearly every name at once: Freeport cut its FY2026 copper and gold guidance on a Grasberg ramp-up delay; Albemarle's Chile workforce voted 97.49% to strike just as a new, not-yet-seated CEO was named; and Lynas confirmed failed takeover talks while its CEO search stretches past five months. USA Rare Earth absorbed 126.85M new shares of dilution to close Serra Verde. Even the sector's most established and most consolidated names are working through operational or leadership disruption, not operating from settled strength.
Policy dependency, now with a hard date
Xi Jinping's Sep 24 Washington visit adds a specific near-term date to a policy dependency that was previously open-ended: rare-earth licensing frictions, the Busan truce's two separate November expirations, and Lutnick's still-overdue Section 232 refined-copper tariff decision (a proposal would phase in a 15% tariff from 2027, rising to 30% by 2028) are all live simultaneously. A visit that resolves the shipment halts would be bullish; one that doesn't leaves the sector exposed into year-end.
