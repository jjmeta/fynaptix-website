03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
SUMMIT ARRIVES
Xi's Washington visit is now three days out
Xi Jinping arrives in Washington Sep 24 and departs Sep 25 — his first White House visit in 11 years, and an unusually compressed one that skips the UN General Assembly entirely. Rare-earth licensing (yttrium, scandium, neodymium, indium) is explicitly on the agenda, but expectations for a transformational agreement are low. The visit lands 47 days before China's expanded export-control suspension expires Nov 10, and 99 days before tougher US defense sourcing rules take effect Jan 1, 2027.
BEIJING CONSOLIDATES
China Rare Earth Group moves on MP's Chinese shareholder
Reuters reported Sep 18 that state-owned China Rare Earth Group is in talks to acquire Shenghe Resources, which holds a 3% stake in MP Materials, and reportedly wants a controlling position. Shenghe has called the reports "false," but if the deal proceeds it would hand Beijing a passive window into a company where the US Department of Defense is the largest shareholder — and better access to the export quotas Beijing uses to control supply.
SUPPLIERS STILL PULLING BACK
Chinese shipment halts remain unresolved heading into the summit
The Chinese rare-earth suppliers who quietly halted shipments to US buyers in early August — wary of Beijing penalties tied to Responsible Minerals Initiative compliance — have not resumed as of this week. The NdPr magnet complex fell together as a block for the first time since May, a sign pricing momentum is cooling even as the supply picture stays tight. China separately placed MP Materials and USA Rare Earth on an export-control list back in June, a reminder the friction predates and could outlast this month's diplomacy.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
Lithium carbonate's global benchmark slipped a further 0.8% to $19,750/t in early September, continuing a decline that has run through most of 2026, and the NdPr magnet complex fell together as a block for the first time since May. Both are reminders that 2026's rare-earth and lithium price strength is not a one-way trade — a producer can be an excellent business at the wrong point in its price cycle.
China can flood the market — or freeze the tap — or just consolidate it
Chinese suppliers are still withholding some rare-earth shipments from US buyers, while Beijing simultaneously moves to consolidate ownership of Shenghe Resources — MP Materials' 3% Chinese shareholder — through state-owned China Rare Earth Group. Investors betting on any direction here are making a bet on Beijing's posture, not just on markets, and that posture is exactly what's on the table at the Sep 24-25 summit.
Policy tailwinds aren't guaranteed to arrive
Freeport's 7-8% plunge on copper-tariff uncertainty (even after its subsequent recovery) shows how much of the sector's bull case rests on policy that hasn't actually been finalized. The same is true elsewhere: MP Materials' 10X plant EBITDA guarantee, Energy Fuels' $725M conditional government support, and Project Vault's $10B EXIM-backed stockpile are all real, but each carries execution and political risk between announcement and cash landing.
Dilution and leadership overhangs compound
USA Rare Earth filed a fresh securities registration Sep 4 on top of the 126.85M Serra Verde shares and is down 54% from its peak; Lynas' CEO search under interim chief Pol Le Roux has now run more than two months, with Amanda Lacaze's transition support ending Sep 30; and Albemarle's new CEO doesn't take over until Feb 2027. None of these is disqualifying on its own, but none is resolved either.
