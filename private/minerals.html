03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
EXPORT CONTROLS
Beijing weaponizing processing dominance
The IEA estimated Jul 16 that full implementation of China's rare-earth export controls could put $6.5T of downstream production at risk across autos, high-tech, defense and energy, with EU licensing approval rates reportedly below 25%. China's Jun 24 MOFCOM Announcement No. 26 (effective Jul 1) formalized enforcement of dual-use strategic-mineral violations, layering on top of the entity-list additions (10 US firms in June, 14 EU firms in July) already in place. China controls roughly 90% of rare-earth processing.
ESCALATION
Truce getting tested on two clocks
The Busan truce (Oct 2025) suspended the Oct 9 export controls for one year and paused gallium/germanium/antimony/superhard dual-use restrictions through Nov 27, 2026, while general rare-earth/battery-material export licenses run through roughly Nov 10, 2026 — two different deadlines Washington and Beijing still need to navigate. China's Jun 22 entity-list additions targeted MP Materials and USA Rare Earth, the two largest recipients of US federal rare-earth investment ($550M and $1.6B), and remain unresolved.
ONSHORING
Washington's playbook, compounding company by company
Project Vault ($10B EXIM loan + ~$2B private capital, EO signed Feb 2) remains the umbrella policy, backstopped by FORGE — the 54-country mineral-alliance forum launched at February's Ministerial, which reports $30B+ in US financing support mobilized. This week the policy translated into company-level closes: USA Rare Earth's $1.55B Serra Verde SPV finished funding (Aug 24, $750M direct from Washington) and Energy Fuels completed its ASM implementation (Aug 28) — onshoring now compounding deal by deal, not just headline by headline.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
The lithium "cycle turn" thesis just met its first real pushback: China carbonate spot slipped 2.18% on Aug 25 to ~$23,299/t as a bearish narrative around mine restarts and a possible supply glut took hold, even after two prior weeks of recovery. Copper shows the same pattern in reverse — a fresh record ($6.703/lb) on Aug 5 had already faded to ~$6.55/lb by late August. Neither commodity has confirmed a durable direction yet.
China can flood the market
Beijing has both weaponized scarcity (yttrium oxide up ~140x since April 2025) and, historically, dumped supply when it suited its interests. Meanwhile the emerging lithium bear case is explicitly a glut story — mine restarts adding supply back into the market — a reminder that ex-China and non-China producers alike are still exposed to commodity cycles they don't control.
Juniors dilute, and even leaders overrun
USA Rare Earth is still issuing ~126.85M new shares to close Serra Verde even after its Q2 revenue miss; Lithium Americas has spent $1.8B of Thacker Pass's $2.93B budget on top of an expensive Yorkville convertible facility. And it isn't just juniors — Lynas, the sector's most established non-China producer, still saw its Malaysia HRE facility capex jump 63% (A$180M→A$294M), missing profit estimates despite record revenue. Building refining capacity is expensive at every stage of maturity.
Policy dependency
Lutnick's copper-tariff recommendation remains overdue past its Jun 30 deadline, with any phase-in possibly pushed to 2027 or later; the Busan truce has two different components expiring on two different dates in November; and China's Jun 22 entity-list additions against MP Materials and USA Rare Earth remain unresolved. Company-level deals are closing (USAR's SPV, Energy Fuels' ASM), but the macro policy catalysts the sector leans on are still open questions.
