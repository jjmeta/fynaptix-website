03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
EXPORT CONTROLS
Beijing weaponizing processing dominance
Ex-China rare-earth prices remain far above pre-controls levels after Beijing's April-2025 move — yttrium oxide near $1,100/kg (~140x), dysprosium oxide ~$1,450/kg, terbium oxide ~$4,500/kg (4-5x) — and shipments of yttrium, dysprosium and terbium into the US remain down roughly 50% versus pre-control levels. China widened controls further in 2026, adding 10 US firms in June and 14 EU firms in July, even as the NdPr alloy benchmark jumped 21.4% in a single month through Jul 1. Trump signed EO 14415 (Jul 20), tightening waivers for defense contractors sourcing from covered nations.
ESCALATION
Truce getting tested on two clocks
The Busan truce (Oct 2025) suspended the Oct 9 export controls for one year and paused gallium/germanium/antimony/superhard dual-use restrictions through Nov 27, 2026, while general rare-earth/battery-material export licenses run through roughly Nov 10, 2026 — two different deadlines Washington and Beijing still need to navigate. China's Jun 22 entity-list additions targeted MP Materials and USA Rare Earth, the two largest recipients of US federal rare-earth investment ($550M and $1.6B), and remain unresolved.
ONSHORING
Washington's playbook, compounding company by company
Project Vault's $10B EXIM loan + ~$2B private capital remains the umbrella policy, reinforced by an Aug 11 White House announcement of $2B+ in new minerals/magnets/battery investment and DOE's Aug 18 award of $162M to nine critical-minerals recovery projects (scandium, copper, antimony, REEs). On top of that, Energy Fuels' ASM implementation and USA Rare Earth's Serra Verde vote both land Aug 28. Separately, Malaysia is weighing an easing of its rare-earth export moratorium and Indonesia resumed exports Aug 3 — early signs of non-China, non-US supply also entering the picture.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
Lithium's turn now has two straight weeks of evidence behind it (China spot up to CNY152,250/t from a 6-month low, Albemarle +11% weekly on its Q2 beat) — but copper already illustrates the flip side: it hit a fresh record ($6.77/lb) on Aug 7 and had pulled back to $6.55/lb by Aug 19. Records don't stay records, and a two-week recovery doesn't yet confirm a durable cycle turn either.
China can flood the market
Beijing has both weaponized scarcity (yttrium oxide up ~140x since April 2025) and, historically, dumped supply when it suited its interests. It also widened entity-list controls to 14 EU firms in July even as Malaysia weighs easing its own rare-earth export moratorium and Indonesia resumed exports — alternative non-China supply emerging that could pressure ex-China pricing from the other direction over time.
Juniors dilute
USA Rare Earth missed Q2 revenue again ($5.82M vs. $8.21M est.), yet the company is still issuing 126.85M new shares (34.1% of the combined company) to close Serra Verde; Lithium Americas has now spent $1.8B of Thacker Pass's $2.93B capex budget on top of the $175M Yorkville convertible facility layered onto its DoE loan. Building refining capacity remains expensive, and names without direct sovereign grants keep diluting to get there.
Policy dependency
Lutnick's copper-tariff recommendation is now nearly two months overdue past its Jun 30 deadline, with any phase-in possibly pushed to 2027 or later; Energy Fuels' $1.9B VAC deal has a newly ambiguous close date; and the Busan truce has two different components expiring on two different dates in November. Multiple policy catalysts the sector is leaning on have all gotten murkier, not clearer, this month.
