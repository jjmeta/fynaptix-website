03 // GEOPOLITICS
The China Factor
EXPORT CONTROLS
Beijing weaponizing processing dominance
Ex-China rare-earth prices remain far above pre-controls levels after Beijing's April-2025 move — European dysprosium still around $2,250/kg (~8x), terbium ~5x, yttrium oxide up to ~140x to ~$1,100/kg — and shipments of yttrium, dysprosium and terbium into the US remain down roughly 50% versus pre-control levels. A May 2026 Trump-Xi summit in Beijing raised hopes of extending the truce, but analysts describe the pause as functionally over already.
ESCALATION
Truce getting tested
China's Jun 22 entity-list additions targeted MP Materials and USA Rare Earth — the two largest recipients of US federal rare-earth investment, at $550M and $1.6B respectively — and remain unresolved heading into the Nov 10, 2026 truce deadline. Coverage increasingly frames multinationals as unprepared for a deeper crisis should Washington and Beijing fail to renegotiate the reprieve.
ONSHORING
Washington's playbook, compounding company by company
Project Vault's $10B EXIM loan + ~$2B private capital remains the umbrella policy; on top of it, Energy Fuels' ASM acquisition won 98%+ shareholder approval Aug 12 (implementation Aug 28), MP Materials' Project Swarm is pre-selling 10X magnet capacity to drone and motor makers, and USA Rare Earth's Serra Verde vote (Aug 28) is now down to its last closing condition.
04 // BEAR CASE
Risks & Counterarguments
Commodity cycles cut both ways
Lithium is showing its first concrete signs of turning — China spot climbed to ¥148,000/t on Aug 12 off a 6-month low, and CRU's Q3 base case is $33,900/t, up ~49% QoQ — but Citi cut its Albemarle price target to $175 from $225 the same week even after an earnings beat. Copper is at record highs too, but one analysis already flags Freeport as "not an obvious bargain" at today's levels. A price spike or a single strong quarter doesn't confirm a durable cycle turn.
China can flood the market
Beijing has both weaponized scarcity (yttrium oxide up ~140x since April 2025) and, historically, dumped supply when it suited its interests; analysts now describe the post-Busan pause as functionally over, with the Nov 10 truce deadline the key swing factor either way.
Juniors dilute
USA Rare Earth's Q2 revenue missed by 27% and shares fell ~9% after hours, yet the company is still issuing 126.85M new shares (34% of the combined company) to close Serra Verde; Lithium Americas has now spent $1.8B of Thacker Pass's $2.93B capex budget on top of the $175M Yorkville convertible facility layered onto its DoE loan. Building refining capacity remains expensive, and names without direct sovereign grants keep diluting to get there.
Policy dependency
Washington's own copper-tariff decision is now more than a month overdue past its Jun 30 deadline; Energy Fuels' $1.9B VAC deal still needs to close behind the newly-approved ASM acquisition; and the onshoring trade broadly leans on a truce Beijing has already tested once this quarter. A policy reversal or a durable détente both cut against the thesis, just in different ways.
