04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
The industry has now committed an estimated $3–4T to AI infrastructure against an annualized return requirement of roughly $600–800B in new profit (up from Sequoia's original $200B framework in late 2023), while actual AI-attributable revenue runs an estimated $50–150B a year — a gap that keeps widening as the top-5 hyperscaler 2026/FY27 capex total climbs to $825–855B. For context, cloud computing's capex-to-revenue ratio at the same point in its adoption curve (2011) was roughly 2.4:1, far below today's multiple. The BIS's June 2026 annual report still names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
Rates are the transmission mechanism — but strength can still win the argument
The 30-year Treasury eased slightly to ~5.36% on Sept 11 after touching its highest level since 2007 — still elevated as AI-linked debt issuance adds to bond-supply pressure. BofA estimates the surge in AI-related corporate debt sales has added roughly 0.3 percentage points to 10-year rates this year, and Nomura pegs hyperscaler borrowing alone at ~25% of net Treasury note/bond issuance to private investors. But Oracle's Sept 10 beat-and-raise, which sent the stock up ~15% on the week, kept the fundamental-demand side of the argument intact — a reminder the debate can swing hard in either direction week to week.
Off-balance-sheet obligations turning concrete
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets, with debt funding of the buildout holding near 32% of capex over the trailing twelve months (FactSet). Oracle remains the starkest case on the balance sheet — still BBB- (one notch above junk) with FY26 free cash flow at -$23.7B and S&P still modeling a ~$42B FY27 deficit — even though this week's Q1 FY2027 results (revenue +30%, Cloud Infrastructure +121%) were genuinely strong, and Larry Ellison canceling a planned $7.5B stock sale reads as a bullish insider signal. The credit risk and the operating momentum are both real; they haven't yet reconciled.
Circular financing
Nvidia is reportedly in talks to anchor-invest up to $10B in Anthropic's own IPO — a chipmaker taking a stake in the public offering of one of its largest customers — on top of the existing $105B OpenAI Ohio data-center financing guarantee. Michael Burry has escalated, calling Nvidia's revenue "majority circularly financed" and adding shorts on Oracle, Micron and Nebius plus March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF. Analysts have tallied more than $800B in criss-crossing investment and purchase commitments across the broader AI supply chain, a pattern CNBC's Jim Cramer publicly compared to dot-com-era financing. Nvidia's Sept 10 "Sovereign Intelligence" tie-up with Palantir extends the web further still, deepening ties between the chip layer and the application layer.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years; bears argue the real economic life is closer to 2–3 years, understating depreciation by an estimated ~$176B across 2026–2028 and flattering reported earnings (Oracle by an estimated 26.9%, Meta by 20.8%, by 2028 on this analysis). Michael Burry has doubled down on shorts in Oracle, Micron and Nebius, plus fresh March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF, calling current pricing "what the top of a boom looks like." Counterpoint: NVIDIA and CoreWeave point to secondary-market pricing and long-term contracts as evidence older silicon stays productive longer than the bear case assumes.
Layoffs while capex soars
Tech-sector layoffs have now crossed 128,536 workers across 299 companies in 2026 through Sept 10 (Layoffs.fyi) — already exceeding all of 2025 — with more than 6,300 cuts in the first ten days of September alone, led by Uber's ~3,300-role reduction. Meta itself reassigned ~7,000 staff into Applied AI this month after cutting ~8,000 (10% of the workforce) in May, and is now reconsidering its own flatter-org structure. The juxtaposition — record infrastructure spend, record layoffs, and organizational whiplash even at companies with strong core businesses — keeps fueling the argument that AI economics lean partly on cost-cutting elsewhere, not purely on AI revenue.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total, and AI took 86% of all US venture dollars in H1 per PitchBook), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage and quantum sentiment at once. NVIDIA now sits at the center of deals spanning chips (TSMC, Broadcom), model hosting (Hugging Face), enterprise software (Palantir) and now a potential anchor stake in Anthropic's IPO — a single company's balance sheet increasingly touches nearly every layer of this page. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters and Oracle's Sept 10 beat is a live example — some strategists call the bear case premature given actual demand signals.
