04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
The industry has now committed an estimated $3–4T to AI infrastructure against an annualized return requirement of roughly $600–800B in new profit (up from Sequoia's original $200B framework in late 2023), while actual AI-attributable revenue runs an estimated $50–150B a year — a gap that keeps widening as the top-5 hyperscaler 2026/FY27 capex total climbs to $825–855B. For context, cloud computing's capex-to-revenue ratio at the same point in its adoption curve (2011) was roughly 2.4:1, far below today's multiple. The BIS's June 2026 annual report still names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
Rates are the transmission mechanism — but strength can still win the argument
The 30-year Treasury has held near 19-year highs (~5.25–5.29% through early September) as AI-linked debt issuance adds to bond-supply pressure, and stocks have chopped session to session on the news (S&P 500 +0.46% one day, -0.4% the next in the first week of September). But Broadcom's Sept 2 beat-and-raise and Dell/HPE's Sept 3 jump on AI-server demand kept the fundamental-demand side of the argument intact — a reminder the debate can swing hard in either direction week to week.
Off-balance-sheet obligations turning concrete
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets, with debt funding of the buildout holding near 32% of capex over the trailing twelve months (FactSet). Oracle is now the starkest case: S&P downgraded its credit to BBB- on Aug 25 (one notch above junk), FY26 free cash flow already came in at -$23.7B, and S&P projects the deficit widening to ~$42B in FY27 even as the company insists it won't add more debt this calendar year — leaning on a $20B equity raise instead.
Circular financing
Nvidia's proposed OpenAI financing backstop remains capped under $120B and covers only the first phase of an Ohio data center — a sign the market's pushback on "vendor financing its own customers" is affecting deal structure. Michael Burry has escalated, calling Nvidia's revenue "majority circularly financed" and adding shorts on Oracle, Micron and Nebius plus March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF. Separately, analysts have tallied more than $800B in criss-crossing investment and purchase commitments across the broader AI supply chain, a pattern CNBC's Jim Cramer publicly compared to dot-com-era financing on July 27. Nvidia's own newly confirmed $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition (Sept 2) extends the web one layer further, from chips into the open-source model-hosting layer its customers depend on.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years; bears argue the real economic life is closer to 2–3 years, understating depreciation by an estimated ~$176B across 2026–2028 and flattering reported earnings (Oracle by an estimated 26.9%, Meta by 20.8%, by 2028 on this analysis). Michael Burry has doubled down on shorts in Oracle, Micron and Nebius, plus fresh March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF, calling current pricing "what the top of a boom looks like." Counterpoint: NVIDIA and CoreWeave point to secondary-market pricing and long-term contracts as evidence older silicon stays productive longer than the bear case assumes.
Layoffs while capex soars
Oracle's latest round of job cuts took effect with the Sept 1 start of its fiscal Q2 — on top of the ~21,000 already cut — even as S&P now models $90–95B of FY2027 capex funded substantially by a fresh equity raise. Microsoft cut roughly 4,800 more in July. Tech-sector layoffs remain near a 20-year high. The juxtaposition — record infrastructure spend, shrinking headcount, and disclosures like Meta's own AI systems breaching internal test environments — keeps fueling the argument that AI economics lean on cost-cutting elsewhere in the business, not on AI revenue itself.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage and quantum sentiment at once. Commentary this week has flagged unusually high concentration among megacap AI names as a risk that could fuel an outsized drop if institutions start trimming winners. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters (Broadcom's Sept 2 beat-and-raise, Dell/HPE's Sept 3 jump on AI-server demand) — some strategists call the bear case premature given actual demand signals.
