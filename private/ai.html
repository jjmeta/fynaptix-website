04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
Hyperscalers are now tracking toward $750–900B of 2026 capex against roughly $51B of direct AI revenue generated — a gap that widened further after Amazon, Alphabet and Meta all raised guidance again in late July. The BIS estimates the top-5 hyperscalers will spend over $1T combined across 2025–2026 and names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
A market that's turned selective, not just skeptical
Alphabet's Jul 22 capex hike helped erase $787B from the Magnificent Seven in a single session on Jul 23 — the group's worst day in over a year. Yet a week later Microsoft (+8%) and Amazon (+10%) were rewarded for the same kind of guidance hike, while Meta fell ~9–10% despite beating on revenue. The market isn't punishing capex outright anymore — it's punishing capex without a visible growth payoff (Azure, AWS) more than capex with one.
Off-balance-sheet obligations
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets. Oracle's own disclosure is a live example: a $638B AI backlog, boosted by a $300B OpenAI deal, of which only ~12% converts to revenue within 12 months — obligations booked well ahead of cash.
Circular financing
Interlocking vendor-equity stakes and take-or-pay compute deals among NVIDIA, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Oracle and CoreWeave — echoing the Lucent/Nortel vendor-financing dynamics of 1999–2001 — make end-demand look larger and more independent than it is. Oracle's $300B OpenAI compute deal, funded in part by an estimated $40B external capital raise, is the latest data point. Analysts now peg the web of circular deals at over $800B, and the BIS flags a "circular financing collapse" as a top-three global financial-stability risk.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years, but bears (Michael Burry among them) argue the chips' real economic life is closer to 1–3 years — an estimated ~$176B understatement of depreciation across 2026–2028 that flatters reported earnings. The first defaults could surface in 2027–2028 as initial lease terms come up for renewal.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (~80% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage, and quantum sentiment at once. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters (TSMC's guide raise, AWS's fastest growth in 18 quarters) — some strategists call the bear thesis premature given actual demand signals.
