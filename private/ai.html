04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
The AI industry is burning roughly $400B a year in infrastructure spend against $50–60B of direct AI revenue generated — a gap that has kept widening as hyperscaler capex guidance keeps rising, pushing the top-4 total to ~$725B for 2026 alone (top-5 with Oracle: $775–800B). The BIS's June 2026 annual report names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
A market that's turned selective, not just skeptical
Chip stocks alone shed more than $1 trillion in a late-July selloff after TSMC's strong print still came paired with a higher capex ask, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) is now down roughly 9.5% from a month earlier even after its blowout YTD run. AMD (record Data Center revenue, still fell 8%), Broadcom (down on an unrelated VMware security scare) and even NVIDIA have all posted down days this month despite good news attached. The market is repricing capex-without-proof, not capex itself.
Off-balance-sheet obligations turning concrete
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets, and FactSet now finds hyperscalers funding an increasing share of the buildout with debt rather than cash — incremental debt has risen from 9% of capex in FY24 to 32% over the trailing twelve months. Oracle remains the live case study: a $638B AI backlog of which only ~12% converts to revenue within 12 months, a credit downgrade to BBB- (near junk), a further round of layoffs reportedly planned for August on top of ~21,000 already cut, and an FY26 cash outflow that ran $23.7B ahead of what the company actually generated.
Circular financing
Interlocking vendor-equity stakes and take-or-pay compute deals among NVIDIA, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Oracle and CoreWeave — echoing the Lucent/Nortel vendor-financing dynamics of 1999–2001 — make end-demand look larger and more independent than it is. Analysts now peg the web of circular deals at over $800B; the BIS flags a "circular financing collapse" as a top-three global financial-stability risk, and even CNBC's Jim Cramer has compared the pattern to the dot-com bubble.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years, but bears argue the chips' real economic life is closer to 2–3 years — an estimated ~$176B understatement of depreciation across 2026–2028 that flatters reported earnings, with Oracle's earnings overstated by an estimated 26.9% and Meta's by 20.8% by 2028 on this analysis. Michael Burry has expanded his short book beyond NVIDIA and Palantir to include Nebius, Micron and the semiconductor ETF, calling current pricing "what the top of a boom looks like." Counterpoint: NVIDIA and CoreWeave point to secondary-market pricing and long-term contracts as evidence older silicon stays productive longer than the bear case assumes.
Layoffs while capex soars
Oracle cut ~21,000 jobs and is reportedly planning another round in August, even as it keeps raising AI infrastructure spend; Microsoft cut another ~4,800 in July. Tech-sector layoffs are running at a 20-year high. The juxtaposition — record infrastructure spend, shrinking headcount, and companies like Meta disclosing their own AI systems breaching internal test environments — is fueling the argument that AI economics are being funded by cost-cutting elsewhere in the business, not by AI revenue itself.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage, and quantum sentiment at once. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters (TSMC's guide raise, AWS's fastest growth in 18 quarters, Broadcom's renewed multi-year Apple deal) — some strategists call the bear thesis premature given actual demand signals.
