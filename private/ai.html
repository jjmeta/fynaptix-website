04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
The AI industry is burning roughly $400B a year in infrastructure spend against $50–60B of direct AI revenue generated — a gap that widened further as Amazon, Alphabet and Meta all raised capex guidance again this summer, pushing the top-4 hyperscaler total to ~$725B for 2026 alone. The BIS's June 2026 annual report names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
A market that's turned selective, not just skeptical
Alphabet's late-July capex hike helped erase ~$787B from the Magnificent Seven in a single session — the group's worst day in over a year. A week later Microsoft and Amazon were rewarded for similar guidance hikes while Meta fell despite beating on revenue; this week AMD beat on record Data Center revenue (+107% YoY) and still dropped over 8%. The market is repricing capex-without-proof, not capex itself.
Off-balance-sheet obligations turning concrete
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets. Oracle is now the live case study: a $638B AI backlog of which only ~12% converts to revenue within 12 months, a credit downgrade to BBB- (near junk), ~21,000 jobs cut (13% of headcount) to fund the buildout, and Wisconsin regulators now demanding a $7B+ collateral guarantee before its flagship data center can even connect to the grid.
Circular financing
Interlocking vendor-equity stakes and take-or-pay compute deals among NVIDIA, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Oracle and CoreWeave — echoing the Lucent/Nortel vendor-financing dynamics of 1999–2001 — make end-demand look larger and more independent than it is. Analysts now peg the web of circular deals at over $800B; the BIS flags a "circular financing collapse" as a top-three global financial-stability risk, and even CNBC's Jim Cramer has compared the pattern to the dot-com bubble.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years, but bears (Michael Burry among them) argue the chips' real economic life is closer to 2–3 years — an estimated ~$176B understatement of depreciation across 2026–2028 that flatters reported earnings. Counterpoint: NVIDIA and CoreWeave point to secondary-market pricing and long-term contracts as evidence older silicon stays productive longer than the bear case assumes.
Layoffs while capex soars
Oracle cut ~21,000 jobs and Microsoft another ~4,800 in July even as both raise AI capex guidance — tech-sector layoffs are running at a 20-year high. The juxtaposition (record infrastructure spend, shrinking headcount, and companies like Meta disclosing their own AI systems breaching internal test environments) is fueling the argument that AI economics are being funded by cost-cutting elsewhere in the business, not by AI revenue itself.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage, and quantum sentiment at once. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters (TSMC's guide raise, AWS's fastest growth in 18 quarters) — some strategists call the bear thesis premature given actual demand signals.
