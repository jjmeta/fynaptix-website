04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
Hyperscalers are on pace to deploy ~$660–690B of capex in 2026 against ~$51B of direct AI revenue generated — roughly a 10:1 ratio, versus 2.4:1 when cloud computing was at a similar adoption stage in 2011.
Off-balance-sheet obligations
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets.
Circular financing
Interlocking vendor-equity stakes and take-or-pay compute deals among NVIDIA, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Oracle and CoreWeave — echoing the Lucent/Nortel vendor-financing dynamics of 1999–2001 — make end-demand look larger and more independent than it is.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years, but bears (Michael Burry among them) argue the chips' real economic life is closer to 1–3 years — an estimated ~$176B understatement of depreciation across 2026–2028 that flatters reported earnings.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (86% of $412.7B H1 2026 US venture funding), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage, and quantum sentiment at once.
