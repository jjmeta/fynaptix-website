04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
Hyperscalers are on pace to deploy ~$660–690B of capex in 2026 against ~$51B of direct AI revenue generated — roughly a 10:1 ratio, versus 2.4:1 when cloud computing was at a similar adoption stage in 2011. The BIS's 2026 Annual Report names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
Capex now punished, not rewarded
Alphabet beat Q2 revenue estimates but fell >7% — its worst day in over a year — after raising 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B; the Magnificent Seven lost 5.7% in a single week as investors stopped rewarding spending increases and started punishing them.
Off-balance-sheet obligations
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets.
Circular financing
Interlocking vendor-equity stakes and take-or-pay compute deals among NVIDIA, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Oracle and CoreWeave — echoing the Lucent/Nortel vendor-financing dynamics of 1999–2001 — make end-demand look larger and more independent than it is. Analysts now peg the web of circular deals at over $800B, and the BIS flags a "circular financing collapse" as a top-three global financial-stability risk.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years, but bears (Michael Burry among them) argue the chips' real economic life is closer to 1–3 years — an estimated ~$176B understatement of depreciation across 2026–2028 that flatters reported earnings. The first defaults could surface in 2027–2028 as initial lease terms come up for renewal.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (86% of $412.7B H1 2026 US venture funding), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage, and quantum sentiment at once.
