04 // BEAR CASE
The Bubble Debate
The revenue gap
Sequoia's own framework for the revenue needed to justify AI capex has risen from $200B (late 2023) to roughly $600B today; actual AI-attributable revenue is estimated at just $50–150B annually (Exponential View pegs the broader generative-AI run rate near $175B) — a 4–13x gap that keeps widening as the top-5 hyperscaler 2026/FY27 capex total climbs to $815–840B. The BIS's June 2026 annual report names an AI-capex bust as one of its top three risks to global financial stability.
Rates are the transmission mechanism — but strength can still win the argument
The Aug 18 rate-driven selloff (30-year Treasury at 5.32%, SOXX -4.96%, CoreWeave -12%) argued a debt-funded buildout is now exposed to financing-cost risk, not just AI-demand risk. But NVIDIA's Aug 26 beat-and-raise ($96.2B revenue, $108B Q3 guide) helped the Nasdaq rally 1.6% the next session, and commentary has already started citing names like Microsoft, TSMC and Cisco as evidence the bubble narrative is losing its best argument — a reminder the debate can swing hard in either direction week to week.
Off-balance-sheet obligations turning concrete
Moody's flags ~$662B of signed-but-not-commenced data-center lease commitments sitting off hyperscaler balance sheets, with debt funding of the buildout holding near 32% of capex over the trailing twelve months (FactSet). Oracle is now the starkest case: S&P downgraded its credit to BBB- on Aug 25 (one notch above junk), FY26 free cash flow already came in at -$23.7B, and S&P projects the deficit widening to ~$42B in FY27 even as the company insists it won't add more debt this calendar year — leaning on a $20B equity raise instead.
Circular financing
Nvidia's proposed OpenAI financing backstop was cut from an originally floated $250B to under $120B and now covers only the first phase of an Ohio data center — a sign the market's pushback on "vendor financing its own customers" is affecting deal structure. Jensen Huang insists the arrangement isn't circular financing, but Michael Burry has escalated, calling Nvidia's revenue "majority circularly financed" and adding shorts on Oracle, Micron and Nebius plus March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF. Nvidia's own reported $12.9B pursuit of Hugging Face (Aug 26) extends the web one layer further, from chips into the model-hosting layer its customers depend on.
Depreciation clock
Hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years; bears argue the real economic life is closer to 2–3 years, understating depreciation by an estimated ~$176B across 2026–2028 and flattering reported earnings (Oracle by an estimated 26.9%, Meta by 20.8%, by 2028 on this analysis). Michael Burry has doubled down on shorts in Oracle, Micron and Nebius, plus fresh March 2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF, calling current pricing "what the top of a boom looks like." Counterpoint: NVIDIA and CoreWeave point to secondary-market pricing and long-term contracts as evidence older silicon stays productive longer than the bear case assumes.
Layoffs while capex soars
Oracle is planning another round of job cuts ahead of the Sept 1 start of its fiscal Q2 — on top of the ~21,000 already cut — even as S&P now models $90–95B of FY2027 capex funded substantially by a fresh equity raise. Microsoft cut roughly 4,800 more in July. Tech-sector layoffs remain near a 20-year high. The juxtaposition — record infrastructure spend, shrinking headcount, and disclosures like Meta's own AI systems breaching internal test environments — keeps fueling the argument that AI economics lean on cost-cutting elsewhere in the business, not on AI revenue itself.
Concentration everywhere
AI is simultaneously the market's largest index weight, VC's largest allocation (OpenAI and Anthropic alone took 43% of a record $510B global H1 2026 venture total), and this hub's connective thread — a drawdown would hit robotics, nuclear, storage and quantum sentiment at once, as the Aug 18 rate-driven selloff already demonstrated across chip names. Counterpoint: infrastructure vendors keep posting record quarters (NVIDIA's actual $96.2B Q2 FY27 print, TSMC's 2nm ramp running ahead of schedule) — some strategists call the bear case premature given actual demand signals.
