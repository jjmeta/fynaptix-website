Michael Burry CASSANDRA UNCHAINED (FORMERLY SCION) BEAR
AUG 4–13, 2026
"The fish have gotten very fat, very large, easy to shoot."
Burry doubled down this week on shorts against Oracle, Micron and Nebius, calling the trade "a bit like shooting fish in a barrel" on his Substack, Cassandra Unchained. He disclosed adding to the Nebius short around $247, Micron around $924 and Oracle around $152, plus more March-2027 puts on the semiconductor ETF. His thesis: AI infrastructure firms are depreciating expensive GPUs too slowly, which he estimates could overstate Oracle's profits by 26–27% by 2028, while locking themselves into huge future lease and hardware payments. Not going entirely his way yet — Nebius jumped 20% in the days right after his comment. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Ray Dalio BRIDGEWATER (FOUNDER) BEAR
RECORDED JUL 30, REPORTED AUG 17, 2026
Today's AI market could be "the biggest investment bubble in American history."
On The Diary of a CEO podcast, Dalio said current AI euphoria shows "classic signs" of a bubble — elevated valuations, concentrated ownership, euphoric sentiment and excessive stock issuance — and put today's setup at roughly 80% of the excess seen before the 1929 crash and the 2000 dot-com top. He pointed to SpaceX's record-breaking IPO and Anthropic and OpenAI's march toward trillion-dollar valuations as exactly the kind of speculative issuance surge that historians treat as a bubble's clearest warning sign. He's calling the odds, not the top.

Stanley Druckenmiller DUQUESNE FAMILY OFFICE FADING BULL
AUG 2026
"Dribs and drabs of AI" — no longer "driving the engine" of the portfolio.
In Morgan Stanley's "Hard Lessons" interview, Druckenmiller said that after three years of a heavily AI-driven book, Duquesne is now finding opportunities in hard assets like copper, and that "macro," dormant for a decade, is coming back. Consistent with his Q2 13F rotation out of chip suppliers into hyperscalers and airlines, but this is the clearest verbal signal yet that even one of the AI trade's most aggressive traders is deliberately diversifying away from it, not abandoning it.

04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Gurley's funding argument, Buffett's price discipline, Son's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
