02 // THIS WEEK
What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Michael Burry CASSANDRA UNCHAINED (FORMERLY SCION) BEAR (DOUBLING DOWN, TIMELINE UNDER TEST)
SEP 28 – OCT 2, 2026
“I want more leverage in my short positions. Better timelines make leverage more palatable.” Days later, on Nvidia’s GPU-depreciation defence: “We have all been here before.”
Per Motley Fool’s reading of his late-September newsletter, Burry swapped shorts for put options expiring June 2027, including Micron (~$500 strike) and Nebius, with further puts on other AI names and index funds, arguing AI capex is increasingly debt-dependent and revenue has to catch up. His Oct 2 post (“Don’t Believe Your Lyin’ Eyes, GPU Depreciation & Useful Lives”) likened current Nvidia rhetoric to computer-leasing stocks in Adam Smith’s 1968 The Money Game. The plain context: two days after the Micron put news, Micron reported fiscal-year results that Streetwise Reports describes as records (FY revenue $133.2bn, Q4 gross margin ~87%), with analysts raising targets. That cuts against his near-term timing, and his bubble-burst date has already moved before; the puts run to mid-2027, so the call is neither proven nor refuted. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Bill Ackman PERSHING SQUARE NEUTRAL (AI WINNER AND DISRUPTOR)
OCT 1, 2026
“What AI has done is massively increased the risk of disruption.” He also said the pace of improvement is “by far the fastest of anything I’ve ever seen.”
On Shane Parrish’s Knowledge Project, as reported by Benzinga, Ackman told investors to ask how AI could disrupt every business they own, and said some businesses will disappear as a result. Yet Pershing Square holds Microsoft, Meta and Amazon, and he called Brookfield “an AI winner for sure” on physical-infrastructure demand. A separate roundup headline attributes “People are Going to Lose a Lot of Money” to him; we haven’t verified that context, so treat it as unconfirmed. His view is a two-sided one: disruption risk for incumbents, upside for those who supply the build-out. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Masayoshi Son SOFTBANK GROUP BULL (UNUSUALLY CAUTIONARY ON SAFETY)
OCT 4, 2026
Superintelligence in the wrong hands could be “super dangerous”: “We don’t have any more luxury of having human fight against human.”
Speaking in Kyoto, Son urged countries to cooperate on controlling superintelligence. The Next Web notes this is an uncharacteristic tone from someone with about $64.6bn invested in OpenAI who has previously dismissed bubble talk. It is a warning about safety and geopolitics, not about valuations or returns, so it does not move his investment stance; his debt-funded buildout (flagged last week at 9.75% coupons) continues. 03 // THE DISAGREEMENT
Where the Real Argument Is
QuestionBull answerBear answer
Micron just posted record results — does that rebut Burry, or is it exactly what a peak looks like?Revenue and margins at these levels, with large customer commitments and analysts lifting targets, are the opposite of an AI slowdown; Burry’s puts expire in 2027 and he needs the data to turn sharply to be rightPeak earnings are what cycles look like at the top; Burry’s argument is about debt-funded capex outrunning revenue later, which a strong quarter now doesn’t address
Ackman says AI “massively increased the risk of disruption” while holding the big platforms — coherent or contradictory?Coherent: the winners are the suppliers and platforms; the risk sits in businesses that AI replaces, which is a stock-picking edge, not a bubble callIf the pace of change is “the fastest of anything I’ve ever seen”, forecasting which incumbents survive gets harder, not easier, and concentration in a few platforms is itself a risk
Berkshire is putting billions into homebuilders instead of AI — a signal or noise?Valuation discipline is a feature: buying a sector 40% off its high at the cycle low is how Berkshire has historically made money, and it doesn’t say AI is wrongMortgage rates near 7.5% and weak builder sentiment mean cheap can stay cheap; it is also one manager’s early-tenure choice, not an AI verdict
Son warns superintelligence could be “super dangerous” while borrowing heavily to fund it — a contradiction?Believing the technology is powerful enough to be dangerous is consistent with believing it is worth owning, and with wanting cooperative governanceThe more transformative and risky the asset, the harder it is to underwrite with debt at near-junk coupons; the safety warning is also a reminder of how uncertain outcomes are
If you can't state the strongest version of both columns, you don't understand the trade yet. That's the real use of this page.
04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Burry's depreciation argument, Ackman's disruption-risk framing, Wood's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
