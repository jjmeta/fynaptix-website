02 // THIS WEEK
What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Michael Burry SCION ASSET MANAGEMENT BEAR
AUG 4–7, 2026
"There's really gold in them thar hills. Just pointing out future ghost towns."
Burry named names: Palantir is "really filling acute CYA needs," Nvidia's "margins & prices really cause nosebleeds," and even Caterpillar's current boom rests on AI-driven demand he expects to fade. His Trading Post updates from Aug 4–7 show him adding to shorts (Tesla, Caterpillar, Applied Materials, Micron among them) and buying more Nvidia puts. Relevant to your AI & Compute bear case — worth noting rival short-seller Steve Eisman publicly called Burry's market-top call "premature" the same week, and Burry has called tops before that didn't arrive on schedule.

Warren Buffett / Greg Abel BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY SHIFTING FROM ABSTAINING
AUG 8, 2026
Cash pile fell to $365.5bn from a record $397.4bn — Berkshire was a net buyer of equities for the first time in years, roughly $20bn in Q2, plus $4.5bn of buybacks.
The clearest move yet from Greg Abel, who took over as CEO from Buffett at the end of 2025, that he's willing to deploy differently than his predecessor. Buffett himself said earlier this year the pullback "wasn't big enough" to move Berkshire's cash — Abel evidently disagreed at the margin. Still, ~$365bn remains on the sidelines: read this as a shift in degree, not a reversal, of the abstaining stance this page has tracked all year.

Masayoshi Son SOFTBANK MAXIMUM BULL
AUG 6, 2026
Record net asset value of ¥72.3 trillion (~$445bn) — even as quarterly profit fell 18%.
At SoftBank's Q1 FY2026 earnings briefing, Son laid out a "No. 1 ASI (artificial superintelligence) platform provider" strategy spanning models (OpenAI), chips (Arm, Ampere), infrastructure (SB Energy), and physical AI/robotics (Wayve, Nuro, AutoStore, Symbotic, plus the pending ABB Robotics deal). Telling reaction: the stock still fell about 4% in Tokyo despite beating estimates, on concern over startup valuation markdowns inside the portfolio. Direct line to your Robotics page.

Ray Dalio BRIDGEWATER (FOUNDER) SKEPTIC
JUL 30 / AUG 4, 2026
"Wealth is not the same as money. You see a lot of people getting wealthy but you can't spend the wealth."
On The Diary of a CEO, Dalio bluntly endorsed permabear Jeremy Grantham's "biggest investment bubble in American history" call ("He's right") and said current conditions show "classic signs" of one. His mechanism: a surge in stock issuance — SpaceX's IPO, plus Anthropic and OpenAI both racing toward roughly $1 trillion listings — combined with rising rates, is exactly what "pricks" a bubble. Sits opposite the case argued on your AI & Compute page.

Cathie Wood ARK INVEST BULL
AUG 6–9, 2026
Bought $21m of Block and $2.3m of SpaceX on Aug 6; added $14.3m more Tesla into the recent slide.
Wood kept buying weakness this week rather than trimming — Tesla is now an $870m position, ARK's largest — and reiterated her call that Bitcoin could reach $1.25m within five years despite its recent downturn. Consistent bull posture even as ARKK's multi-year underperformance keeps drawing scrutiny (Motley Fool ran a "still a buy?" piece the same week).

Jim Chanos CHANOS & CO BEAR (SELECTIVE)
LATE JUL 2026
"We have the same setup" as the dot-com crash — "closer to a '99-type moment than a '97."
At Global Alts New York, Chanos argued for owning "what the chips produce, not where the chips reside" — long Nvidia and TSMC, short the "neocloud" data-center landlords like CoreWeave (9.35x sales, -41% ROE) and Nebius that he says are equipment-leasing businesses priced like software companies. He's also short SpaceX, which he called a "hopes and dreams IPO," and Tesla. Expects the reckoning "as early as" late 2026 into 2027.

AUG 3–7, 2026
Built Microsoft into ~15% of the portfolio (~$2.4bn) on the view the market isn't pricing Microsoft's 27% OpenAI stake — which he estimates near $200bn, or 7% of Microsoft's market cap.

04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Gurley's funding argument, Buffett's price discipline, Son's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
