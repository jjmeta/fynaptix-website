Two names move meaningfully this week, in opposite directions. Stanley Druckenmiller goes from a chip-specific rotation to an outright macro call: at a closed-door Piper Sandler conference (Sep 10-11) he told attendees the market is "very likely in an earnings bubble" and said Duquesne has cut its AI-related exposure to roughly 20% of what it was six months ago — a real de-risking, not just reshuffling (53% → 38%). But he isn't leaving the trade: he flew to Korea on Sep 22 specifically to meet Samsung, SK Hynix and Doosan/Doosan Enerbility, wagering that memory and power infrastructure are the next bottleneck worth owning even if the broader AI trade is overbuilt. Warren Buffett / Greg Abel move the other way: Berkshire just broke a 14-quarter streak of net stock selling, buying a net ~$20bn in Q2, roughly $17bn of it Alphabet stock bought into Google's own AI-capex-funding secondary offering — the clearest signal yet that Abel's Berkshire is willing to underwrite the AI buildout where Buffett's wouldn't (55% → 64%). Masayoshi Son keeps pushing further into maximum-bull leverage, but at a rising price: the jumbo dollar-and-euro bond flagged here last edition actually priced this week, at $11.1bn across five tranches — with the longest dollar tranche costing SoftBank 9.75% a year, well above investment-grade levels, funding continued stakes in OpenAI and Anthropic. No new disclosed moves this week from Chanos, Dalio, Laffont or Coleman.

What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Stanley Druckenmiller DUQUESNE FAMILY OFFICE BEAR (CUTTING EXPOSURE, BUT ROTATING NOT EXITING)
SEP 10–22, 2026
Markets are "very likely in an earnings bubble" — and Duquesne has cut AI-related exposure to about 20% of its level six months ago.
Remarks at a closed-door Piper Sandler conference on Sep 10-11, reported by the Financial Times; he separately called U.S. rates "at best too low" and Fed officials' claims that policy is restrictive "absurd." This is a step beyond the chip-specific reshuffling covered here in prior weeks — a direct statement that the broad market is overpriced. But the follow-through complicates a simple bear read: on Sep 22 he traveled to Korea to meet Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and Doosan/Doosan Enerbility, targeting HBM memory and power-infrastructure suppliers as bottleneck plays that benefit even if the broader AI capex cycle disappoints. He's shrinking the bet, not walking away from it. Relevant to your AI & Compute page and Nuclear & SMRs page.

Greg Abel BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY NEUTRAL → TURNING BUYER
DISCLOSED SEP 27, 2026
Berkshire bought $23.5bn and sold just $3.7bn of equities last quarter — a net ~$20bn purchase that ends 14 straight quarters of net selling. About $17bn of it went into Alphabet stock, bought into Google's own secondary offering raised to fund AI spending.
Paraphrased from Motley Fool's read of the newly reported 13F. One quarter doesn't make a trend, and Berkshire's own cash pile is still enormous, but this is Abel's clearest fingerprint yet on the portfolio he now runs — a willingness to underwrite AI-capex-adjacent bets that a more skeptical, price-disciplined Buffett-era Berkshire sat out. Worth watching whether this becomes a pattern in Q3. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Masayoshi Son SOFTBANK GROUP BULL (LEVERAGE GETTING PRICIER)
PRICED SEP 27, 2026
Priced $11.1bn across five USD/EUR bond tranches to keep funding SoftBank's OpenAI and Anthropic stakes — including a 7.5-year, $4.5bn dollar tranche at 9.75% and a 5.5-year, $4.5bn tranche at 9.25%.
Paraphrased from bond-market reporting. This is the jumbo issuance flagged as "marketing" in last edition's spectrum note, and it actually priced — but at rates well above what an investment-grade borrower would pay, on top of a separate $40bn one-year bridge loan for the Anthropic stake reported the same week. Bloomberg frames it plainly: Son's debt-fueled AI bet is becoming more expensive. He's still doing it anyway, which is either conviction or the clearest sign yet of how much this strategy now costs to keep running. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Druckenmiller's bottleneck logic, Wood's convergence thesis, Slater's headcount argument. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
