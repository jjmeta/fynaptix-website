02 // THIS WEEK
What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Ray Dalio BRIDGEWATER (FOUNDER) BEAR (SHARPENING)
SEP 9, 2026
"We are now in a bubble."
Posted on X, this drops the hedge Dalio has used since January, when he called AI merely the "early stages" of a bubble. His argument: nearly every major technology wave — railroads, the Industrial Revolution, the 1920s convergence of electricity, refrigeration, telephones, radio, airplanes and cars — produced a bubble before it produced lasting value, and AI's convergence with robotics, energy and biotech looks the same. He isn't calling the technology fake; he expects it to prove valuable eventually. The claim is narrower and more useful: current prices already assume the best outcome, for companies nobody has yet sorted into winners and losers. Relevant to your Macro page.

SEP 9–11, 2026
"This will be an interesting market this fall" — closed out his Nvidia and Palantir December 2026 puts and trimmed the rest of his book, saying he's "happy to" sit in cash for now.
Paraphrased from two Substack "Trading Post" updates. This is the site's most prominent bear pulling back, not capitulating: he still says by his own account he expects a rough fall and remains short Palantir on paper, but he's choosing to reduce size and hold cash rather than press the bet into uncertain near-term timing. It lands the same week Dalio's rhetoric got sharper — the two most bearish names on this page moving in different directions on conviction versus caution. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Masayoshi Son SOFTBANK GROUP BULL (LEVERAGING UP)
SEP 8–17, 2026
SoftBank executives are meeting U.S. investors in New York this week to gauge demand for a jumbo dollar junk-bond sale that would fund still more of Son's AI buildout.
Paraphrased from Bloomberg and Japan Times reporting. CFO Yoshimitsu Goto and colleagues are holding meetings Sept. 14–17, arranged by Citi, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley. SoftBank just reported a 5-trillion-yen ($31bn) annual profit — Son's response is to raise more debt rather than bank the gains, continuing the borrowing spree that's already funded stakes in OpenAI, Arm and AI infrastructure. It's the clearest maximum-bull expression on this page: levering an already-profitable balance sheet further, betting AI returns keep outrunning the cost of the debt — and the direct mirror of the risk Dalio's bubble call is describing. 44% of institutions cut their "Magnificent Seven" holdings in Q2 while 42% added or initiated — with 36% of all filers still net buyers of AI infrastructure names like CoreWeave, Arista Networks and Broadcom.
Paraphrased from a Hedgeweek analysis of 6,371 pension funds, hedge funds and wealth managers. A market this large with zero institutional consensus on direction is itself worth sitting with, whichever side you lean toward. Relevant to your Macro and AI & Compute pages.

04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Gurley's funding argument, Buffett's price discipline, Son's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
