Michael Burry's rhetoric sharpens even though his early-September portfolio moves suggested caution: a pair of Substack/X posts (Sep 14 and Sep 16) reject the AI-as-AGI premise outright and frame the entire financing structure as a wealth-transfer mechanism — pulling him back toward maximum bear (7% → 4%). What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Michael Burry CASSANDRA UNCHAINED (FORMERLY SCION) BEAR (SHARPENING)
SEP 14–16, 2026
"LLMs are not AI and won't be AGI. There is nothing AI to slow down." … "I am righteously indignant over the wealth transfer to the very few that these bubbles create."
Two posts on X and his Substack, Cassandra Unchained. This is a step up from his early-September portfolio de-risking (closing Nvidia and Palantir puts, moving to cash): rather than just managing risk around uncertain timing, Burry is now making a structural argument — that "circular financing among tech companies" and "schemes emanating from Washington DC" are driving "extreme enrichment of the lucky few," which he calls "hemlock to the health and longevity of the free Republic." Separately, he argues large language models are categorically incapable of becoming AGI, meaning there's no capability slowdown to debate — only, in his view, a wealth-transfer machine. Masayoshi Son SOFTBANK GROUP BULL (LEVERAGING UP)
SEP 4–20, 2026
Priced Japan's largest-ever corporate bond — a ¥1 trillion ($6.3bn) retail note at 4.75% — on Sep 4, and is now marketing a further jumbo dollar-and-euro bond to help fund SoftBank's next OpenAI investment, expected to close next month.
Paraphrased from Bloomberg and Japan Times reporting. This is the follow-through on the investor roadshow flagged last edition: the retail bond actually priced, at the high end of its guided 4.3%–4.9% range, and the larger institutional tranche — reportedly around $10bn in dollar notes plus roughly €1.1bn in euro notes, arranged by Citi, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley — is moving forward. Son is stacking a second layer of debt specifically to keep funding an illiquid private stake, the clearest maximum-bull, maximum-leverage expression on this page. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Cathie Wood ARK INVEST BULL (ROTATING)
SEP 14–19, 2026
Sold $12.8M of Alphabet; bought $28.1M of Meta as Mark Zuckerberg's "superintelligence" push takes shape; put $3.35M into flying-taxi maker Archer Aviation.
Paraphrased from ARK's daily trade disclosures. DISCLOSED SEP 17, 2026
Sold Duquesne's entire Broadcom stake and more than 11x'd its Amazon position, while also trimming Micron and Intel, according to newly reported Q2 filings.

That's a step back from the concentrated "AI infrastructure picks-and-shovels" trade without being a bear call on AI itself — a different move from Tepper's, below, who's doing roughly the opposite. Peter Thiel THIEL MACRO NEUTRAL (POWER, NOT CHIPS)
DISCLOSED SEP 11, 2026
After reporting zero U.S. stock holdings for two straight quarters, Thiel Macro re-emerged with a $419M portfolio that's 72% energy and power names, plus a $118M stake in Amazon.
Paraphrased from the newly disclosed Q2 13F, covered by Motley Fool and Yahoo Finance. This is a specific, legible bet rather than a directional call on AI broadly: Thiel's fund is betting the next scarce input in the AI buildout is electricity generation and grid capacity, not compute — the Amazon stake reads as a proxy for AWS's own data-center power demand rather than a bet on AI software. A different angle on the same buildout that Druckenmiller and SoftBank are also positioned around, above. Relevant to your Nuclear & SMRs page and Energy Storage page.

03 // THE DISAGREEMENT
Where the Real Argument Is
QuestionBull answerBear answer
Is dumping concentrated AI-chip exposure (Druckenmiller: Broadcom, Micron, Intel) discipline or missing the trade?Tepper is doing the opposite — sitting 40% in Amazon, Micron and TSM, still adding CoreWeave and SpaceX — and concentrated AI exposure has been the best-performing bet of the yearDruckenmiller has a long history of selling into strength before a drawdown; exiting Broadcom after it hit an all-time high looks exactly like the discipline a bubble unwind eventually rewards
Is Thiel's pivot to power and electricity the smarter next AI trade, or does it just avoid the real question?If the AI buildout is capped by grid capacity rather than chip supply, power generation captures value chips can't — and Thiel's fund sat out two full quarters before making this specific, narrow betBetting on the picks-and-shovels of the picks-and-shovels is exactly the kind of second-order speculation that obscures who's actually capturing real returns, which is Burry's underlying complaint
Does Burry's claim that "LLMs are not AI and won't be AGI" matter for the trade, even if he's right?It doesn't have to be AGI to be profitable — hyperscalers and model labs are already monetizing current-generation systems at scale, and returns don't require the capability curve to keep bending upwardCurrent valuations are explicitly pricing continued, near-exponential capability gains toward AGI-like systems; if Burry's technical ceiling is correct, today's multiples have no support underneath them
Does SoftBank stacking a second jumbo bond on a freshly priced ¥1 trillion retail bond validate the AI capex cycle, or repeat the pattern bears warn about?SoftBank just posted a $31bn annual profit and investors are showing real demand for its debt at these levels — leverage funding a working, profitable strategy is how growth companies have always scaledStacking debt on debt specifically to keep funding one illiquid private stake is the textbook mechanism by which a leveraged bull market turns into a forced-seller bear market
If you can't state the strongest version of both columns, you don't understand the trade yet. That's the real use of this page.
04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Gurley's funding argument, Buffett's price discipline, Son's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
