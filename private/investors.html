02 // THIS WEEK
What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Ray Dalio BRIDGEWATER (FOUNDER) BEAR (MACRO, RATES-DRIVEN)
OCT 7, 2026
AI is a “classic bubble”: “We’re in the part of the cycle that is before that but approaching that… I think we’re close to that.”
Speaking at the Forbes Global CEO Conference in Singapore, as reported by Bloomberg, Dalio pointed to a specific trigger: large amounts of debt raised to fund AI colliding with rising interest rates, plus holders of paper wealth being forced to sell to raise cash (“the bubble usually pricks at that”). In January he said AI was in “the early stages of a bubble”, so this is a clear escalation in tone. He gave no trading instructions and the reporting says nothing of his own positioning, and a bubble call with no date has a long record of being early. Rates are a live variable: the same Bloomberg page shows the 10-year Treasury near 5.27%. Relevant to your AI & Compute and Macro pages.

Cathie Wood ARK INVEST BULL (ADDING AI INFRASTRUCTURE, AIR, DEFENCE)
OCT 7, 2026 (TRADES)
Action, not a quote: ARK bought 214,589 CoreWeave shares across ARKK and ARKW, plus Joby, Archer, Kratos, AeroVironment and Recursion, while trimming Robinhood and DraftKings.
Per Benzinga’s read of ARK’s daily trade disclosures, the day also included about 1.88m Recursion Pharmaceuticals shares, 433,331 Joby, 161,214 Archer, 209,379 Kratos and 31,040 AeroVironment; sales were 151,903 Robinhood and 247,616 DraftKings. The article carries no direct Wood quote, and the headline links the Robinhood trim to a Bitcoin slide, which is Benzinga’s framing, not ARK’s. Our juxtaposition, not ARK’s: CoreWeave is a heavily debt-funded AI-compute provider, exactly the kind of borrowing Dalio is worried about. ARK’s record includes deep drawdowns, so size and timing deserve caution. Relevant to your AI & Compute, Defense Tech and ARK Invest pages.

Michael Burry CASSANDRA UNCHAINED (FORMERLY SCION) BEAR (TIMELINE UNDER TEST)
OCT 2, 2026 (CARRIED OVER)
On Nvidia’s GPU-depreciation defence: “We have all been here before.”
Carried over from last week because it is still the sharpest single bear argument: his Oct 2 post compared current Nvidia rhetoric with computer-leasing stocks in the 1960s, and his late-September newsletter described put options on Micron and Nebius expiring June 2027. Streetwise Reports notes Micron then reported record results (FY revenue $133.2bn per that report), which cuts against his near-term timing. The puts run to mid-2027, so the call is neither proven nor refuted, but his bubble date has already moved once. No new Burry commentary found this week. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Bill Ackman PERSHING SQUARE NEUTRAL (WINNER AND DISRUPTOR)
OCT 1, 2026 (CARRIED OVER)
“What AI has done is massively increased the risk of disruption.”
On the Knowledge Project podcast, reported by Benzinga, Ackman said AI improves faster than anything he has seen and that investors should ask how it could disrupt every business they own. Pershing Square nonetheless holds Microsoft, Meta and Amazon. His view is two-sided: risk for incumbents, upside for the suppliers and platforms. Nothing new from him found this week. 03 // THE DISAGREEMENT
Where the Real Argument Is
QuestionBull answerBear answer
Dalio says debt plus rising rates is the trigger — is that the right risk?The biggest spenders fund AI largely from cash flow; earnings and demand (record memory results, for instance) are still rising, so rates alone don’t break the cycleDalio and Druckenmiller (who said in September that US rates are still “a little low”, per the Financial Times via TheStreet) both point to financing cost; AI spending is increasingly debt-funded and a 10-year near 5% tightens that
ARK is adding CoreWeave while Dalio warns about AI debt — who is reading it right?Compute is scarce and demand is real; owning the infrastructure early is how earlier platform shifts paid, and drawdowns are the price of that exposureCoreWeave-type businesses are leveraged to AI capex continuing; if financing tightens they are first in line, and ARK’s history includes deep drawdowns
Micron just posted record results — does that rebut Burry, or is it what a peak looks like?Revenue and margins at these levels with analysts raising targets are the opposite of a slowdown; Burry’s puts expire in 2027 and he needs the data to turn sharplyPeak earnings are what cycles look like at the top; his argument is about debt-funded capex outrunning revenue later, which a strong quarter doesn’t address
Ackman says AI “massively increased the risk of disruption” while holding the big platforms — coherent?Coherent: suppliers and platforms win, the risk sits in businesses AI replaces, which is a stock-picking edge rather than a bubble callIf change is the fastest he has seen, forecasting which incumbents survive gets harder, and concentration in a few platforms is itself a risk
If you can't state the strongest version of both columns, you don't understand the trade yet. That's the real use of this page.
04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Burry's depreciation argument, Ackman's disruption-risk framing, Wood's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
