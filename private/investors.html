02 // THIS WEEK
What They're Saying
Recent public commentary and moves, most consequential first. Refreshed every Monday.
Warren Buffett / Greg Abel BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY SHIFTING TOWARD BUYING
AUG 8–12, 2026
"That doesn't mean that investing is terrible. It does mean that prices for an awful lot of things will look very silly."
Q2 numbers, confirmed this week: Berkshire bought $23.5bn of equities and sold just $3.7bn — a $19.8bn net buy, its first net-buying quarter in more than three years after 14 straight quarters as a net seller. About half of that was the previously disclosed $10bn Alphabet stake. Cash still fell only modestly, to $365.5bn from a record $397.4bn, and Buffett's own framing above — delivered around the same earnings window — is hardly a bull call. Read this as Abel finding selective value inside a market its own chairman calls silly-priced, not a reversal of the abstaining stance this page has tracked all year.

Michael Burry CASSANDRA UNCHAINED (FORMERLY SCION) BEAR
AUG 4–10, 2026
"We are near a major top, and possible a 1987-type fall."
Burry — who deregistered Scion with the SEC last year and now posts through his Substack, Cassandra Unchained — wrote this on Aug 4 even as the S&P 500 jumped 1.9% to a fresh record close, arguing new highs will just pull in more money before the drop. He's refusing to cover shorts in Nvidia, Tesla, Palantir, Micron, Caterpillar, Applied Materials and the semiconductor ETF, with puts against Nvidia and Palantir now roughly 80% of his disclosed book. His one long conviction call: a paired bet on Flutter and DraftKings, on the view that prediction-market regulation is coming. Direct counterpoint to Berkshire above, and relevant to your AI & Compute page.

Stanley Druckenmiller DUQUESNE FAMILY OFFICE BULL (ROTATING)
AUG 14–15, 2026
Q2 13F: completely exited Broadcom, Intel and Micron — the same AI-chip basket he'd just built in Q1 — while boosting Amazon by over 1,000% and initiating new stakes in AMD and Alphabet.
Paraphrased from the filing, not a direct quote. Duquesne also added crypto-mining infrastructure names Bitdeer and Riot Platforms, plus fresh stakes in Delta and United Airlines. Since his March entry, Intel and Arm had already run up over 190% — Druckenmiller took the win and rotated into hyperscalers instead of chip suppliers. Still an AI-bullish portfolio overall, but the whiplash between quarters (buy the semis, then dump them four months later) is itself a data point on how fast even top allocators are repositioning.

Cathie Wood ARK INVEST BULL
AUG 4–10, 2026
Sold roughly $21m of Palantir right after its 29% earnings-day jump, then bought $26.6m more Nvidia on Aug 10 along with fresh CoreWeave, Cerebras, SpaceX and Block additions.
Also trimmed ~$96m of Roblox. Net effect: Wood took profits on the AI-application names that had run hardest and rotated further into AI compute and infrastructure — a bet on the picks-and-shovels layer over the apps layer, not a reduction in overall AI exposure. Consistent bull posture even as ARKK's multi-year underperformance keeps drawing scrutiny. Relevant to your AI & Compute page.

David Tepper APPALOOSA MANAGEMENT BULL (CONCENTRATED)
AUG 14, 2026
Appaloosa is up 32% in the first half of 2026, driven by memory-chip makers, and its Q2 13F shows it narrowing to single-name conviction: Amazon, Micron, TSMC, Alphabet and Uber are now its top five holdings.
Tepper closed out 12 positions entirely in the quarter, including Microsoft, SanDisk, JD.com and Lyft. Read alongside Druckenmiller above, the pattern among some of the sharpest macro traders isn't retreat from AI-linked names — it's narrowing sprawling thematic bets down to the handful they have the highest conviction in, which cuts against the idea that "smart money" is quietly heading for the exits.

Jim Chanos CHANOS & CO BEAR (SELECTIVE)
LATE JUL / AUG 2026
"We have the same setup" as the dot-com crash — "closer to a '99-type moment than a '97."
At Global Alts New York, Chanos argued for owning "what the chips produce, not where the chips reside": long Nvidia and TSMC, short "neocloud" data-center landlords like CoreWeave that he says are equipment-leasing businesses priced like software companies, plus a short on SpaceX, which he called a "hopes and dreams IPO." That short has had a rough two weeks by his own framing — SpaceX shares rallied 23% after last week's lockup expiration and closed back above their $135 IPO price on Aug 10, though they remain well off their post-IPO peak. Worth holding both facts at once.

04 // HOW TO USE THIS
Reading Famous Investors Without Getting Hurt
Survivorship bias is enormous
You hear from the ones who were right. The equally confident managers who blew up don't get interviewed. Fame is evidence of a good run, not of a repeatable process.
Their constraints aren't yours
Burry can hold puts that expire worthless; Berkshire can sit in cash for years; Son can lose $10bn and continue. Your timeline, tax position and tolerance are different — copying their positions without their balance sheet is how people get hurt.
13F filings lag by up to 45 days
Anything sourced from filings shows long US equity positions only — no shorts, bonds, derivatives or overseas holdings — and may already have been sold. Treat it as archaeology, not a signal.
BETTER USE
Mine the reasoning, not the ticker
The valuable part is why — Gurley's funding argument, Buffett's price discipline, Son's convergence thesis. Test those arguments against your own sector pages, then log where you land in the Decision Journal.
BEST USE
Find who disagrees with you
If everyone on this page you find persuasive sits on the same side of the spectrum, that's a warning about your reading habits rather than a confirmation of your thesis.
