The Edinburgh long-horizon growth house — and the UK's main route to private AI

01 // WHY THIS ONE MATTERS TO YOU
The Practical Case
It's the accessible route to companies your hub already tracks
SpaceX, Anthropic, Databricks and Stripe appear across your AI, Space and IPO Pipeline pages as private companies you can't buy. Scottish Mortgage holds all four. It's a FTSE 100-listed investment trust, so unlike US-domiciled ARK funds it's directly purchasable through any UK platform — no PRIIPs problem, no FX wrapper. That combination is rare, and it's the main reason this belongs alongside ARK rather than beneath it.

03 // THE PHILOSOPHY
How Baillie Gifford Thinks
ASYMMETRY
A few winners pay for everything
Their core belief: stock returns are radically skewed — a tiny number of companies generate almost all long-run market gains. So the job is finding those few and holding them through volatility that would shake out most managers.
TIME ARBITRAGE
Patience as the edge
They argue their advantage isn't better information but a longer horizon — willingly holding for five to ten years while the market prices the next quarter. Structurally the same bet ARK makes, executed with less turnover.
PRIVATE ACCESS
Buying before the IPO
Unlike most ETFs, the trust structure lets them hold unquoted companies. SpaceX was bought in 2018 — eight years before its June 2026 Nasdaq listing — which is where a large share of the recent return came from.
VS ARK
Same family, different temperament
Both bet on innovation over a decade. Baillie Gifford trades less, holds more private assets, and has had the better recent run; ARK is more thematically explicit and far more transparent about daily trades. Reading both is more useful than picking one.

05 // WHAT TO UNDERSTAND FIRST
Investment Trust Mechanics
Trusts behave differently from ETFs in ways that matter. This isn't covered on your Investing 101 page yet — worth learning before considering one.
PREMIUM / DISCOUNT
Price ≠ net asset value
A trust's shares trade independently of the underlying portfolio. Buying at a 10% discount means paying 90p for £1 of assets — and that gap can widen against you. Always check the discount before buying, never just the price chart.
GEARING
Trusts can borrow
Unlike most ETFs, trusts may use leverage to boost returns — which amplifies losses equally. Check the gearing level in the factsheet.
Valuation of private holdings
Unquoted stakes are valued periodically, not by a live market — SpaceX itself only became a live market price in June. With the position now down to about 15% of assets post-listing, and other names like Anthropic (4.0% of assets, reportedly targeting a November IPO at a reported $1.8tn or more against a last confirmed private mark of $965bn) and Databricks still marked privately, a single revaluation or IPO price still moves the whole trust.
Concentration is the strategy and the risk
SpaceX sat at 15.0% of assets on the 30 Sep factsheet (latest published, and probably lower since SPCX fell about 10% in the past week) after easing back from a post-IPO peak near 25-26%, per Canaccord Genuity — and shareholders only narrowly endorsed lifting the private-asset ceiling further at July's AGM (24.1% voted against). That single position drove the outperformance and remains the dominant single risk, with limited ability to trim it during the staggered post-IPO lock-up (releases run through Q3 results, with founder Elon Musk restricted for 366 days). Both facts are the same fact.
06 // HOW TO READ IT
Honest Scorecard
The recent record is strong
Beating the FTSE All-World by ~9 points in the year to March, then watching that private position actually IPO as the biggest listing in history, is a real vindication of the long-horizon approach — rolling 12-month NAV return has since accelerated further to +36.3% (to 30 Sep). Anthropic, now 4.0% of the trust, is reportedly aiming for a November IPO, which would be the next big test of the private marks (no public S-1 yet and timing is not confirmed). The last week is a reminder the other way: the Nasdaq sold off, SPCX fell about 10%, and the discount widened.
But one holding did the work
Attributing a firm-wide philosophy to what was substantially one extraordinary position is exactly the reasoning error the Calibration page exists to catch. Ask what the return looks like without SpaceX — and note the discount has widened this week to about 9.3-9.5%, against a 6.35% 12-month average, even as buybacks keep running (635,000 shares on 15 Oct) and the share price hit a record at the end of September; the market isn't fully buying the buyback story. The five-year share-price return is only +13.9% against +79.3% for the index, a reminder of how much the 2021-22 drawdown still weighs. Canaccord Genuity's 'hold' rating and a Seeking Alpha downgrade both cite the same concentration this page flags, though Canaccord also frames a 5-10% discount as broadly reasonable given the risk — this one now sits at the top of it.
It has been brutal before
The same strategy produced a severe drawdown in 2021–22 when rates rose. Anyone considering it should look at that period, not just the last twelve months — the strategy hasn't changed, the environment did.
Best used as a second opinion
Where Baillie Gifford and ARK hold the same names, that's consensus among long-horizon growth investors. Where they diverge — and where the bears disagree with both — is the interesting research question.
