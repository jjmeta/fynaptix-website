Reading the backdrop

Rates (US10Y) The gravity on growth stocks Rising long yields compress valuations of long-duration cash flows — quantum, humanoids, pre-revenue SMRs and biotech feel it first and hardest.
VIX Risk appetite gauge Sustained spikes drain speculative sectors regardless of fundamentals — also historically the best moments to deploy the cash "opportunity" sleeve.
Gold Hedge confirmation Gold strength alongside equity weakness is diversification working; gold and stocks rising together often signals inflation or currency concerns.
Bitcoin Liquidity thermometer BTC tends to front-run liquidity shifts — weakness there often precedes pressure on the most speculative equity sleeves.
Dollar (DXY) Overseas earnings & commodities A strong dollar pressures commodity prices (minerals) and international revenue (Japanese FA names); a weak dollar is a tailwind for both.

This week's commentary

The regime shift is now official: the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.1% on Sept 23 — its highest level in 19 years, back to 2007 territory — as markets price in another possible Fed hike on top of the Sept 16 move to 3.75–4.00%, with an estimated ~$300B of AI-linked investment-grade debt issuance for 2026 still adding pressure on top of the Fed's own move.

Equities mostly shrugged it off: the S&P 500 gained roughly 1.2% on the week and sits about 0.7% below its all-time high, though breadth stayed thin beneath the surface — a narrow, mega-cap-led advance rather than a broad rally. The VIX eased toward the mid-14s as post-hike uncertainty resolved.

Crypto told a different story: bitcoin pulled back to roughly $84,000 — about a third below its 2026 peak, though still up an estimated ~43.5% for the quarter — while ether held near $2,690. Read as a liquidity-sensitive cooldown after a strong quarter, not yet a broader risk-off signal, but worth watching given how far speculative sectors on this hub have run.

Beneath it, the stance board saw 18 changes this week: Symbotic, Kratos, Energy Fuels, Lithium Americas, Rocket Lab, Planet Labs, Eos Energy, NVIDIA, Recursion, Alphabet and IonQ were all raised on converting contracts, regulatory de-risking or partner-validated milestones (Deere was also added fresh to Watch in Robotics), while AeroVironment, Rheinmetall, BWX Technologies, Freeport, Redwire and Illumina were trimmed — mostly on a stock that had already run past its own good news, not a single scandal.

The starkest single data point: Freeport-McMoRan's copper hit a fresh all-time high near $6.83/lb, pushing Freeport's market cap up 92% year-over-year — yet the stock now sits right at the average analyst target, with essentially zero implied upside left. Illumina posted the mirror image in biotech, up another 7.45% into a GuruFocus fair-value gap of roughly 105% — two names cut to Watch this week for the same underlying reason.

For the week ahead: the September jobs report (Oct 2) is the single biggest data point for the Fed's next move, with JOLTS, ADP, the final Q2 GDP read and ISM Manufacturing PMI setting up the runway into it. Xi Jinping's Washington visit is behind us but its "relief, not resolution" outcome on rare earths keeps that trade live, and Anthropic's pre-roadshow IPO meetings continue toward a target before the November midterms.
